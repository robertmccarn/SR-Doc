    Generational Chaos

    Preserved source

    {{WIP}}'''Generational Chaos: The Cyclical Phenomena of American History''' is a 2071 book by Dr. Yazmin Bhattaru, expanding on her 2066 essay "The Next American Crisis." In the book, Bhattaru argues that American history tends to follow cycles of economic and social-political development punctuated by crisis moments coinciding with the development of each generation.

Bhattaru offered several deviations from the Strauss–Howe generational theory and the Friedman generational theory, but most significantly was her thesis on how institutional cycles would last much longer as a result in the increase in human lifespans. Claiming the current cycle that began around 2020 would last 100 years, not the usual 80. And the next cycle would most likely last closer to 200 years.

# Highlights
## '''Economic Cycles'''
Bhattaru held that the United States experiences an economic cycle beginning with the emergence of one generation during the height of their economic independence after establishing themselves in the workforce, usually in their 30s and 40s.

## '''National Cycles'''
The book's most significant contribution to sociology lied in its deviation from contemporary analysis of institutional, cultural, or as she called it, "National cycles." Bhattaru concurred with previous analyses from the likes of Strauss and Friedman that historically National cycles last about 80 years beginning and ending with a generation that left their adolescence during the start of the crisis and largely died by the end. Bhattaru argued that paradigm is no longer in effect, as the average lifespan as of 2077 had been extended to 105 years. She argued that this would extend the next National Cycle at least 20 years beyond where it would normally start, and would end less as a result of the death of the Millenials, and more as a result of them being replaced as the largest generation by simple population growth.

## '''Predictions'''
Bhattaru famously predicted with startling accuracy the coming [Second Depression](1446-second-depression.md), identifying the year it would occur, the depth of its impact on the job market, and ultimately how it would be resolved.

She also predicted the inevitability of the United States relationship with Mexico, however in this regard she made some notable errors, such as her claim that the US-Mexican Cold War would be punctuated by only one direct conflict between both powers, and while she correctly assessed that it would be the most destructive war in living memory, she believed the United States would loose the war after the American space colonies become independent.
