    Hivvir

    Preserved source

    The '''Hivvir''' are one of two intelligent inhabitants of the planet Ivrilu in the Kepler-452 system, the other being the [Hiilo](2103-hiilo.md). The Hivvir are natural gliders, with winglike lateral extensions of skin called patagia that allow them to soar great distances in the low gravity, higher pressure atmosphere of Ivrilu.

# Evolution
Ivrilu has a surface gravity only 63% that of Earth, and 125% the atmospheric pressure, enabling far more avenues for evolution to enable powered flight. The Hivvir's distant ancestors--and the common ancestors of all "vertebrates" on Ivrilu-- were six limbed amphibious cephalopods featuring gliding membranes. Later descendants of these ancestors evolved a more tetrapod-esque body plan, with two of the limbs becoming vestigial, but the membranes remained.  

The Hivvir, like the Hillo are bipedal, standing roughly 1.9 meters tall and are noticeably more slender than humans, with no hair or other integument to speak of. They posses eyesight far superior to natural humans, with eyes that can move independently of each other and possess seven color receptors compared to human's natural three. 

# History
## Society
The Hivvir are organized into interconnected tree or cliff dwelling family groups. These troops are capable of using stone hand tools, and express their history and culture through wood carving and cave art, but no troops have been found with a written language. It has also been observed that the Hivvir have a far more rudimentary spoken language than their Hiilo cousins, attributed to smaller brain cavities.

## '''First Contact'''
At the time of First Contact, the Hivvir and Hiilo had fairly limited contact with one another, with the Hivvir proving to be the more difficult of the two species to communicate with. Efforts to give the Hivvir access to terran education have been extremely difficult, with some scientists suggesting that the only way the Hivvir will ever achieve any kind of written civilization will be if they're [artificially uplifted.](3635-uplifted-animals.md)
