Sushi and Apple Pie

Sushi and Apple Pie was an autobiographical book by Carla Anderson, describing her entry into politics, her term as President of the United States, and particularly her complex views regarding World War III. The title refers to her marriage to Heather Suzuki, a desendant of Japanese immigrants who was one of the military personnel killed in the Thanksgiving Attacks.

Anderson acknowledged the necessity of the war, and to that extent supported it, but she was staunchly opposed to Evalyn Jacobi's censorship of the media during the war years to remove "enemy influences", which she saw as a racist attack on free speech. Anderson's views resonated strongly with many who had protested the censorship of Japanese media from American Japanophiles who held underground meetings and protest marches throughout the war. 

Throughout the book, a recurring theme is Anderson's lamentation of the human instinct for violence. She describes how, as a child, she grew up believing that the days of global conflict were over, but how she was proven wrong time and time again. She also, however, discusses the steps toward peace she was able to make during her own term as President, such as the annexation of the Anglosphere into the United States, the First Contact with the K'lerin, and the reopening of trade with Japan in the 2060s. Finally, she takes time to criticize the actions of then-current President Oscar Lachman, which she believed would lead to a Second Mexican-American War. 

The book has remained a classic among left-leaning intellectuals, particularly young adults, well into the 22nd century. It is sometimes even joked (with very little exaggeration) that no Liberal Arts student's dorm room is complete without a copy.
