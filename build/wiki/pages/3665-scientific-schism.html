Scientific Schism

The Scientific Schism, also known as the New Oriental Schism, or the Schism of 2101 (Latin: Magnum schisma scientificum), was a split within the Catholic Church lasting from 2101 to 2133 in which bishops residing in Rome and Licheń both claimed to be the true pope. The schism was driven by blowback from Third Vatican Council the and the earlier Proclamation of Sapere aude. There was also a political element to the schism with the Licheń papacy being closely associated with the Polish government and the See of Rome being much closer to Mexico. These rival claims to the papal throne damaged the prestige of the office.

The schism was finally resolved when the Licheń claimant Pius XIII called the Council of Seville (2130–2134) after the death of Pope Adrian VII. The Council arranged the renunciation of the Licheń antipope Pius XIII, renounced many of the positions of the Third Council while maintaining the Biblical translations compiled by that council, and elected John-Paul III as the new pope reigning from Rome.

See of Licheń[]

The See of Licheń saw a more direct relationship with its patron government in Poland, with Pope Pius XIII overseeing the beginning of each session of the Sejm and swearing in each President and Prime Minister.

The Licheń church also sought to gather legitimacy within the broader Intermarium by inducting local armored infantry mercenary groups as official religious military orders. Three most distinct being the Order of Brothers of the German House of Saint Mary in Brandenburg (the New Teutonic Order), the Order of Saint Jadwiga, and the Order of Saint Stephen.

See of Rome[]

The resurgence of military orders in the Intermarium, which coincided with the new era of warfare centered around armor-wearing troopers ever since the Third World War, who are seen as modern knights, soon spread across Europe. The Rome church eventually did the same as well in response to the Licheń church, seeing such actions as highly provocative and even heretical. Various religious orders allegiant to the See of Rome and its pope, many of which were originally military orders of knights during the Crusades of the medieval period nearly a millennium prior, saw their historic, original roles and duties reestablished, become religious military orders once again.

However, unlike their counterparts in the Licheń church, the Rome church’s military orders inducted less armored infantry mercenaries in comparison. Instead, they relied more on religiously devoted armored troopers volunteered from the Latin American Union and the Mexican Sphere, especially Mexico, as well as their own wealth, members, and volunteers to build up their own armored forces in the process. Four most distinct and well-known being the Order of Brothers of the German House of Saint Mary in Jerusalem (the original Teutonic Order, with no relations to the New Teutonic Order in the Intermarium), the Order of Knights of the Hospital of Saint John of Jerusalem (the Knight Hospitaller), the Order of Christ and of the Temple of Solomon (the New Knights Templar), and the Order of the Holy Sepulchre.

While aren’t a religious military order themselves, the Pontifical Swiss Guard too was also transformed into an armored military force. Besides their age-old ceremonial duty as honor guard, the Swiss Guard still maintained its long-held duty of protecting the See of Rome and its pope. In their function as bodyguards, they are equipped with modern armors used by both the Swiss Army’s armored corps and various Rome-allegiant military orders. Similar historically, volunteers to the Swiss Guard must come from Switzerland, albeit with some differences due to the modern progressive society of the 22nd century unlike in the past. Recruits to the guards must be unmarried Swiss Catholic males and females (instead of just males like historically) between 19 and 30 years of age who have completed not just basic training but also officer and armored infantry training (instead of just basic training like in the past) with the Swiss Armed Forces.
