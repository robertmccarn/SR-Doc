Eighth Party System

The Eighth Party System refers to the era of American national politics that began in 2080 with the rise of the Neo-Jeffersonian Coalition under President Oscar Lachman following the Second Depression. It followed the Seventh Party System which started under Dylan J. Price. The Eighth Party System ended in 2132 with the rise of the Union Party under Lionel J. Halvidar resulting in the creation of the Ninth Party System.

The perceived Eighth Party System is characterized by an electoral shift from the electoral coalitions of the Seventh Party System during the New Nationalist Era: the Conservative Party became the dominant party in the East and major cities while the Progress Party increasingly started to assemble a coalition of Hispanics, rural Progressives, and Augments. A critical factor was the major transformation of the political system in the Lachman Era of the 2080s and beyond, led by Oscar Lachman, during the Second Depression. Dominant personalities during this period included Oscar Lachman (C), Rayleen Malakar (C), Tom Steele (C), Stewart M. Li (C), Miriam Weinstein (P), and Thang Levin (C).

Economic History[]

The eighth party system came on the heels of the Second Depression. As general purpose AIs, longevity technologies, and advanced robotics replaced existing workers or at least reduced the demand for new ones, the US unemployment rate began to steadily climb, wages fell, and the dollar's value began to deflate. By the election of 2080 the US unemployment rate had climbed to 22%,the highest in more than 150 years. The Lachman administration argued for lowering interests rates to free up currency, close the borders and actually deport a sizable portion of America's immigrant population to reduce the strain on the job market, deregulate investment to create more enterprises and therefore jobs, and lower taxes.

These reforms resulted in a majority of Americans forming Personal Liability Corporations by 2095, and the largest expansion of the private banking industry in a century as private equity banks emerged to finance individual purchase of robotic labor for new enterprises, and private investment was decentralized as value shifted from an abstract corporation to individual worth. The resultant economic boom that followed saw Americans leverage this expansion of available investor capital and new energy sources from the colonies to expand the robotic workforce as a means of carrying out personal business objectives.

By 2120 this system resulted in the wealth gap widening to its largest degree since the Labor Shortage, with 99% of the country's wealth being concentrated in the hands of 1% of the population.
