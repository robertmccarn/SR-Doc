Tropical Antarctica

Tropical Antarctica was a book by Malcolm Moreau that presents a hypothetical ecosystem of what Antarctica might have been like 150 million years ago in the Late Jurassic period, a time period where no fossils were found in Antarctica. It is arranged as a "field guide", containing profiles of numerous fictional species based on those known from surrounding parts of the world at contemporary times. The book's success established Moreau's career as an author of speculative-science books, and made him a household name.

Species profiled in the book include the following:

Teratovenator ferox: A 30-foot theropod related to the South American Piatnitzkysaurus, and the apex predator of Antarctica. It is capable of bringing down prey of virtually any size, from fish to other dinosaurs.

Antarctoposeidon jacksoni: An 85-foot, 45-ton sauropod descended from the cetiosaur family. The only sauropod native to Anatrctica, and the continent's largest land animal by far.

Brevidactylus osteofragus: A turkey-sized flightless pterosaur with atrophied wing-fingers whose closest relatives are basal dsungaripterids. It is an omnivore, but its heavy beak is capable of crushing bones.

Lagodon gregarium: A basal ornithischian descended from dinosaurs such as Chilesaurus, which has evolved into a four-legged deer-sized browser.

Nycterotherium nosferatus: A volaticothere (a member of a lineage of Mesozoic mammals with gliding membranes) that has evolved powered flight, and feeds on blood much like a vampire bat.

Thalassotitan ahabi: A gigantic filter-feeding ichthyosaur, the largest animal in the world, that breeds along Antarctica's coasts. At 100 feet long, it rivals the blue whale in size.
