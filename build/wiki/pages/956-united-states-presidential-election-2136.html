The United States presidential election of 2136 was the 88th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 6, 2136. In this match, incumbent Unionist president Lionel Halvidar ran for reelection against Conservative-Union candidate Lydia Monroe, who tried to portray himself to the voters as the "peace candidate" who wanted to bring the Third Mexican-American War to a speedy end. Sarima Haden ran on behalf of the Progressive "Peace-wing," after Halvidar secured the endorsement of the Progress Party. Halvidar was re-elected president by a landslide, partly helped by the admission of several new states from Mars and Luna.

As Mexico had annexed the rebel states of the South West, no votes were counted from any of the eleven southern states that had joined the United States of Aztlan.

Halvidar won with 81.5% of the popular vote on the first ballot, a record only ever beaten by James Monroe and George Washington who both ran unopposed. Halvidar's victory was partly the result of the recent victory at the Battle of Washington, where Halvidar took the field and directly commanded troops during the final hours of the battle. Halvidar's popularity had surged after the victory of DC, and the war had begun to turn in America's favor as a result of the colonies admission into the war.
