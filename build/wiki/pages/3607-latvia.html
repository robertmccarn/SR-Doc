Latvia

Latvia was a Baltic Sea nation independent from 1918 to 1940 and again starting in 1991.

Throughout the 20th century, Latvia was under control as a part of the Soviet Union, which consisted of many nations and held a large influence across the globe. In the 1980's, the decline of the Soviet Union and rising nationalism allowed Latvia and other countries to gain independence following the fall of the Soviet Union.

Latvia quickly integrated itself with the rest of Europe. Joining the European Union and Eurozone, Latvia prospered as a rich country for much of its pre-Flood existence. However, the county suffered economically as the core and capital of the county, Riga, flooded. Unable to build a sea wall, the capital moved to Daugavpils. Meanwhile, Latvia managed to annex territory belonging to the former Russian Federation after its collapse in the 2020s.

As Europe began to collapse from the Flood, Latvia integrated itself with Poland, then a rising power. After Poland's victory in WWIII, it held a level of superiority not seen in Europe since the days of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth more than 4 centuries ago. Rising nationalism and the retreating of the sea levels during the Flood led to Poland annexing Latvia. Despite this, the Federalist Party in Poland gave the Latvians more representation by re-forming Poland into the Federation of Poland and the Baltic in 2076.

Poland's era of strength came crashing down in the 2130s, after the loss of Poland in the Third Mexican-American War. This war gave America a level of total superiority across the globe not seen since WWIII in the mid 2050s. All of Europe entered the American domain, and as the European Association formed, Latvia was granted as an official province of the EA. Today, Latvia remains a province within the Association.
