Podcopters

"Podcopter" is a colloquial name used for various small, usually electric VTOL aircraft used for a variety of functions, including personal transport, medical evacuation, law enforcement, and as taxis. In short, they represent the closest technology has yet achieved to the so-called "flying car" ideal of the 20th century, although there are significant differences: podcopters do not require a physical pilot (they fly autonomously), and most are only large enough to seat two or three people along with their luggage.

The first podcopters were developed in the late 2010s, and were essentially scaled-up versions of the multi-rotored helicopter drones that had become ubiquitous by that point. By the early 2020s, a number of major cities had introduced "drone taxi" services, with the aircraft traveling along preprogrammed routes to transport paying passengers. Soon afterwards, this was expanded to include other uses, with police departments, hospitals, and even a number of private owners purchasing the vehicles, which had by now been termed "podcopters."

Because podcopters are automated, they are much less prone to accidents than manned aircraft, even when flying in close formation. Most podcopters are powered by four or more electric rotors, which can swivel in multiple directions to allow for extreme agility. The podcopter, along with the car, remains a fixture of transportation in cities around the world in the 22nd century.
