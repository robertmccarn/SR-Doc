AGWEST

Army Group West, abbreviated as AGWEST and officially known as the 22nd Army Group, was a United States Army army group formed on the West Coast of the United States during the White Tide. It was one of two army groups created to fight the National Defense Force, the other being Army Group East (AGEAST).

History[]

Formation[]

Like AGEAST, AGWEST was formed in response to the MAGA government's declaration of secession following the 2028 Presidential election.

Interstate campaigns[]

AGWEST's primary objectives were the control of the I-5, I-15, and I-25 highway corridors that would allow supplies to move between Federal positions in the West Coast and the Rockies.

Drive on the Southwest[]

The Fourth Army of AGWEST quickly secured the major cities of Las Vegas, Phoenix, and Santa Fe, encountering only light resistance from the NDF and militant groups. The rapid advance is also attributed to the Fourth Army's abundant access to mechanized transportation in the form of electric vehicles and the vast solar and wind farms in the Southwest that kept such vehicles powered.

Battle for Stevens Pass[]

The Tenth Army of AGWEST was heavily engaged with dislodging NDF forces at Stevens Pass in the Northern Cascade Mountains. Combat lasted for six months, with Seattle's power supply kept intact after a daring airborne operation at the Grand Coulee Dam. The battles allowed the Tenth Army to gain a considerable amount of experience in mountain warfare and asymmetric warfare.

Canadian operations[]

AGWEST nominally assumed command of most Canadian forces in British Columbia and Alberta after the government failed to adequately respond to the Christian nationalist uprising in Alberta. The combined force pushed the Army of West Canada into the Canadian Rockies, cutting off the NDF from its supply line in the north.

Post-MAGA insurgency[]

AGWEST continued counter-insurgency operations following the official surrender of the MAGA regime. It was heavily involved with fighting the American Redoubt in Idaho and Montana, with Tenth Army battalions and SOF teams fighting the Redoubt into the Rocky Mountains and to their final stand in the town of Salmon, Idaho.

Units[]

During its existence, AGWEST controlled two field army-level commands: the Fourth Army, officially the Fourth United States Army, and the Tenth Army, officially the Tenth United States Army. It also worked with Canadian forces in the western part of Canada.

Fourth Army[]

The Fourth Army was tasked with counter-insurgency operations in the Southwest. Sparsely populated and with a light NDF presence, the Fourth Army was considered to have an easy tour of duty compared to other units and field armies.

Tenth Army[]

The Tenth Army was formed from units of the California and Oregon National Guards who had recently defeated NDF forces in Redding. It would spend the majority of the war as a low priority unit fighting the NDF in the Cascade Mountains in Washington.

Canadian forces[]

With the Canadian government unable to respond to Christian nationalist uprisings in Alberta, AGWEST assumed control of most of the Canadian military forces in British Columbia and Alberta.

Personnel[]

When formed, AGWEST was primarily composed of Third Army veterans, National Guard units, and fresh civilian recruits. Civilian recruits, while motivated, were often hastily trained and deployed to guard the Cascade-Sierra line or patrol the I-5 corridor. Regular Army and National Guard personnel were tasked with directly fighting the NDF in offensive operations and securing the Interstate corridors.
