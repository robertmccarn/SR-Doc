Trans-Pacific Forum

The Trans-Pacific Forum, or the TPF, was an intergovernmental military alliance established in 2022. At it's peak, it consisted of 6 pan-Pacific nations, as well as the United States and Canada. Governed by a council made up of the 8 members chaired by the Secretary General, the alliance constituted a system of collective defense, whereby its member states agreed to mutual defense in response to an attack by any external party.

The Trans-Pacific Forum was originally a regional Trade Pact. It grew out of the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership and the proposed Trans-Pacific Partnership, which had been weakened yet continued after the withdrawal of the US under Donald Trump. After the Late 2010s Recession began, member states looked for a means of improving economic growth. The Free trade zone was scaled back somewhat, and more was included to promote cooperation of infrastructure. Both agreements were superseded in favor of the Trans-Pacific Forum treaty in 2022 which included the Chinese while the US and the Americas remained observer states, adopting common shipping practices and collaborating on select infrastructure programs, but largely opting out of the Free Trade Zone. As the Japanese became more assertive they eventually withdrew and in 2044 the US pushed for the TPF to adopt a common security agreement with the remaining member states, which subsequently did so.

The TPF was disbanded after the Third World War, being succeeded by the larger Pacific Defense Organization, and Australia and New Zealand becoming free associations to the United States.
