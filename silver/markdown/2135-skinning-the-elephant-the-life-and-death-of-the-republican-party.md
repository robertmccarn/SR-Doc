# Skinning the Elephant: The Life and Death of the Republican Party

## Preserved source

```wikitext
'''Skinning the Elephant: The Life and Death of the Republican Party''', by Susan Tuttle, was a nonfiction book that dealt with factors that led to the demise of the Republican Party. The books covers the sequence of events running from the commencement of the Sixth Party System with the election of Ronald Reagan to the presidency, to Donald Trump's historic defeat in the 2020 Presidential election due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The book was published in 2024, four years after Trump left the Presidency, and came to be seen by many as a definitive text on the downfall of the Republican party and the rise of a new form of conservatism in America. 

In particular, the book noted how the "free-for-all" culture provided by the internet allowed the implicit racism historically favored by conservatives to become explicit for the first time in decades, and how this had the double effect of alienating the more moderate among them while building up a fanatical base of loyal radicals. According to Tuttle, this reliance on a fringe demographic was ultimately the cause of the party's downfall, as it was faced with mounting scandals and criticism (primarily regarding Donald Trump's relationship with Russia) that Democrats were able to properly take advantage of in 2020 and use to court a wider base. 

Tuttle's final argument was that conservatism in America would not go extinct with the demise of the Republican party, but would simply revive itself on in the form of a new party. This turned out to be true, as the [Conservative Party](310-conservative-party-united-states.md)was founded less than a decade later.
```
