# Independence
Quebec's unique culture and relationship with the rest of [Canada](1896-canada.md) was a constant driver of various independence movements for much of its history. However, at no point prior to the [Flood](637-the-flood.md) was Quebec's relationship with Canada proper so detrimental that majority support for independence could be achieved. 

When the South Greenland Ice Sheet fell into the Atlantic, the floodwaters swept over the Laurentian Plateau and devastated the peninsula. The major population centers in Southern Quebec experienced more flooding than was expected, but the destruction of Quebec's natural resources crippled the regional economy. Economic relief from Canada was mostly going toward rebuilding the Maritimes which were hit even harder than Quebec. Despite preparations and government campaigns to prepare the public for the effects of the Flood, the Quebecois saw themselves as abandoned by the government in Ottawa.

Demonstrations and general public outcry led to elections being held in late 2026 where the Coalition Avenir Québec-Parti Québécois coalition won a super-majority in the National Assembly of Quebec, and the Canadian Parliament permitted a referendum for independence. Polling initially indicated that a narrow majority of Quebecois were in favor of remaining in Canada. However, as the resulting famine of 2027 took hold and the global economy [crashed](642-market-crash-of-2027.md), public support shifted in favor of independence.

On November 13, 2027, the Quebec Referendum was held and the province's citizens voted 64% in favor of independence. Quebec would formally leave Canada on June 24th, 2028.
