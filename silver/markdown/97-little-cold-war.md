# Little Cold War

## Preserved source

```wikitext
The '''Little Cold War''' also known as '''Cold War II''', and the '''Second Cold War''' (approx. 2008–2029) was a state of ongoing tensions, hostilities, and political rivalry between the countries and partners of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO), such as [United States](217-united-states-of-america.md) and members of the [European Union](1255-european-union.md), against the [Russian Federation](https://second-renaissance.fandom.com/wiki/Russian_Federation) and (to a lesser extent) the [People's Republic of China](2418-people-s-republic-of-china.md). The Second Cold War, unlike the First Cold War, this conflict was largely over regional security issues, rather than an overarching economic and ideological conflict.

The official start date of the Second Cold War is often debated, but most historians agree that it began in earnest in 2008 with the Russian Invasion of Georgia. Much like the first Cold War, the second was ultimately about Russia's relationship with the rest of Europe, particularly with respect to its lack of natural borders in the East European Plain that had been exploited by every western invader since Napoleon. Unlike the previous Cold War, this conflict also took into account Russia's population crunch and European dependency on Russian oil and natural gas.

Resources proved to be Russia's greatest advantage during the conflict, and under the leadership of Vladimir Putin the country was able to build up capital from the exportation of natural resources. The Russian dominance over Europe's energy sectors finally proved to be effective at leveraging most of Western Europe and therefore [NATO](1367-north-atlantic-treaty-organization.md) from engaging further with the conflict in the mid 2020s, resulting in the start of the alliance's decline. Only the Eastern European members remained involved in the conflict, being heavily supported by the United States via technology transfer and military aid following the radical rift that had emerged in the alliance.

[Poland](939-poland.md) gradually became the greatest ally of the United States during this period and grew to become one of the most powerful economies in Europe by its end. Second only to Poland were the Baltic states, which sought to align themselves closer to the United States to prevent Russian domination from returning to the region.
[thumb|300x300px|European Alliances after most Western Europe's withdrawal from NATO.](98-file-little-cold-war-png.md)
While Europe played the largest and most visible part of the Second Cold War, other regions saw proxy conflicts develop between factions most notably in Syria. After around a decade, the Syrian government, along with their Russian and [Iranian](2120-iran.md) allies fought off rebels, the Islamic State, and Kurdish-led militias. The complex international series of alliances would lead the conflict to become frozen until Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022 and then dissolved in 2029.[thumb|220x220px|Member states of the Eurasian Union](99-file-eurasian-union-png.md)
[The Russian Federation formally collapsed on August 14, 2029](1577-dissolution-of-the-eurasian-union.md), resulting in the formal end of the Little Cold War and Russia itself.

# Beginnings of the Little Cold War (2008–2014)
## '''Georgia'''
Relations between the two ex-Soviet countries of Russia and Georgia had been strained for years by 2008 as Georgia's pro-Western government had come to power during the Rose Revolution and Georgia was pushing to receive status as a member of NATO in recent months. In addition, Russia was also seeing Kosovo declare unilateral independence from Serbia in the February of 2008. A diplomatic crisis had emerged in April, when Russia announced that it would no longer be participating in sanctions implemented by the Commonwealth of Nations against Abkhazia. Furthermore the Russians would establish direct relations with the Abkhaz and the South Ossetians. The crisis reached its boiling point when the Georgian Army was deployed to South Ossetian conflict zone on August 7 to restore order to the region and put an end to the shelling that had started just a week earlier. Within hours, the separatist stronghold of Tskhinvali was taken by Georgian forces. Russia, having already sent soldiers illicitly across the border to advance into the South Ossetian conflict zone would meet the "Georgian aggression" with military force. Declaring their actions as a peacekeeping operation, Russian and South Ossetian forces proceeded to engage Georgian forces until they had retreated from the region. A second front of the conflict was opened up in Abkhazia after Russian and Abkhazian forces successfully attacked the Kodori Gorge. Georgia was subsequently blockaded by the Russian Navy. The Russian Air Force would attack targets not just within the conflict zone, but in undisputed Georgian territory. In addition, the first implementation of cyber warfare coincided with military action commenced, and so did an information war begin between both sides. Within five days, a ceasefire had been implemented when it became clear that Georgia had been defeated thoroughly by the combined forces of Russia, South Ossetia, and Abkhazia. Russia temporarily maintained an occupation of the cities of Zugdidi, Senaki, Poti, and Gori until they withdrew on October 8. As a result of the war, Georgians had been expelled from the Kodori Gorge and throughout South Ossetia. The Russian military established a presence in the newly recognized states South Ossetia and Abkhazia.

## '''Russian "Reset"'''
The new Obama administration would see try to see the reversal of tensions that had begun under the Bush administration. Both Obama and Medvedev spoke warmly towards each other during the 2009 G20 London summit and promised a fresh start in Russia–United States relations in a joint statement. A symbolic gag took place in March 2009, where Secretary of State, Hillary Clinton, and Minister of Foreign Affairs, Sergey Lavrov, pressed a "reset" button despite the Russian translation being incorrect. In July of 2009, Obama stated that America had interests in seeing Russian prosperity, but Vice President Joe Biden would contradict this by stating that America may strengthen its hand in order to prevent a Russian sphere of influence being established. In April 2010, the New START was signed by representatives of both countries, restricting the amount of long-range nuclear weapons and replacing START I. During the 2011–2013 Russian protests, Russian Prime Minister, Vladimir Putin, alleged that the United States had interfered with the 2011 Russian legislative election and supported protests by the opposition. Following this, the reset in relations was exposed as insincere and relations began to break down. Relations had remained sour as issues of democracy in Russia and Eastern Europe's integration with the West could not be resolved during the period.

## "'''Renewal" of Tensions'''
Two months following Putin’s victory in the 2012 Russian presidential election, tensions restarted after Russia threatened to preemptively strike missile defense sites in order to pressure NATO to scrap their plans to establish said sites. In July, two Tupolev Tu-95 Bears were intercepted by NORAD fighters in the air defense zone off of the coast of Alaska. In August, it had been discovered that Akula-class submarines conducted a patrol, undetected, in the Gulf of Mexico, raising concerns about American capabilities in anti-submarine warfare. In mid-December, the Magnitsky Act was passed and Russia retailpirated by forbidding American citizens from adopting Russians. Hours before the 2013 State of the Union Address, a pair of nuclear-armed Russian strategic bombers circled Guam until they were intercepted by F-15 Eagles dispatched from Andersen Air Force Base. During the global surveillance disclosures, Edward Snowden sought asylum in Russia, which further aggravated relations between both countries. Later in the year, the Obama administration drew a redline for the use of chemical weapons in the Syrian Civil War after the Ghouta chemical attack. The Senate authorized the use of military force in the conflict, but Russia successfully negotiated a deal in which Syria’s chemical weapons stockpile would supposedly be eliminated.

# Crisis and escalation (2014–2020)
## '''Ukraine'''
The events which would lead to the period of renewed hostility between Russia and the Western world began with the Ukrainian crisis. When Ukrainian President, Viktor Yanukovych suspended plans to implement an association agreement with the European Union under pressure from the Kremlin to maintain Ukraine's close ties with Russia, a massive wave of demonstrations and severe civil unrest occurred, which came to be known as the Euromaidan. This eventually culminated with a full-blown revolution which toppled Yanukovych and the Ukrainian government. A new interim government was set up by the revolutionaries and the association agreement was signed. The new government was initially unpopular among Eastern Ukrainians and Russophiles, sparking a period of demonstrations known as the Russian Spring. During this time, the Kremlin perceived that the Maidan Revolution was hostile to Russian interests in Ukraine. With Russia's strategic interests under threat and seeing that pro-Russian sentiment was great in the east of the country, Russia intervened in the crisis and it appeared conflict had begun between the Russian and Ukrainian governments.

In the days following the revolution, Russian Spetnaz swiftly moved to defend the Sevastopol Naval Base through occupying Crimea, seizing government buildings along with blockading military bases. The so-called "little green men," named for unmarked green military uniforms, captured the Crimean Parliament on February 25. Crimea declared independence from Ukraine on March 11. After an controversial referendum on March 16, in which Crimea overwhelmingly voted to join the Russian Federation, Russia proceeded to annex Crimea through an ascension treaty signed on March 18. Russia's actions were subject to international condemnation and is often seen as the beginning of the Little Cold War.

Pro-Russian protests in Eastern Ukraine continued in Donetsk, Luhansk, and Kharkiv. By April 2014, a war had begun in the regions of Donetsk and Luhansk with separatists proclaiming the establishment of the Donetsk People's Republic and the Luhansk People's Republic. Ukraine's Anti-Terrorist Operation initally went badly due to poor preparation and extreme caution stemming from Russian forces massing on the border. During the operation, Donetsk and Luhansk attempted to unify under a confederation which would be effectively abandoned following the downing of Malaysia Airlines Flight 17. Meanwhile fighting escalated in May 2014 with increased efforts by Ukraine to combat the separatists. The Raid of the 95th Brigade saw the rapid advance of Ukrainian forces, pushing Novorossiya to the edge of collapse. Russian attempts at hybrid warfare had failed and to save the situation, Russia began an incursion into Ukraine to support separatist forces in reversing Ukraine's advance. With Russia throwing full support behind the separatists, Ukrainian counter-offensives failed to make a great impact on the battlefront. The subsequent Minsk Protocol signed in September 2014 failed to stop ceasefire violations. Minsk II, signed in February 2015 led to many observers regarding the conflict as having been frozen. This was confirmed as fighting largely died down.

The conflict remained largely frozen for the next 6 years until Russian troops began a massive buildup on the border of Ukraine over the course of 2021. This was likewise accompanied by Russian efforts to delegitimize the government of Ukraine through alleging Russophobic and genocidal intentions towards Russian-speakers in Eastern Ukraine. Western analysts began sounding the alarm that Russia was planning a large-scale invasion. These fears were confirmed when Russia mounted a large invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 with Putin claiming that Russia was conducting a "special military operation" to demilitarize and denazify Ukraine. Western governments began sending military aid to Ukraine and Russia's actions saw international condemnation. A full-scale invasion followed in which Russian forces attempted to rapidly decapitate the government of Ukraine. However, these efforts failed thanks to Ukraine holding off the initial Russian assault on Antonov Airport. The following weeks saw combat across Ukraine with Russian efforts in the Northern front at Kyiv, Chernihiv, and Sumy being foiled by logistical and supply issues, forcing their withdrawal by April 2022. Meanwhile, the Southern front saw early Russian successes at Kherson. However, further Russian advances were halted at Mykolaiv and Voznesensk. The Siege of Mariupol would drag on all the way until May 2022. Attention shifted to the Eastern Front where despite Russia's victory at Izium, their efforts to seize Kharkiv also failed. Donbas once again became the focus of military attention as Russia advanced and seized Sievierodonetsk and Lysychansk. However, Ukraine managed to executed further counteroffensives, exploiting a breakthrough in the Kharkiv Oblast and routing Russian forces. Despite this, Russia continued with their goal of annexing the territories they were present in through holding a referendum in the oblasts of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, and Kherson. Following the annexations, Ukrainian efforts continued focusing on Kherson in the south, and offensives began eating away at the logistics and morale of the Russian defenders of Kherson. By November, the Russians were forced to begin a panicked retreat across the Dnieper River. Ukrainian forces entered the city by mid-November.

Warfare continued into the winter season of 2023, with a stalemate settling in. Fighting occurred along the frontline but was particularly fierce in the ongoing Battle of Bakhmut, which would indicate the sort of fighting which would dominate the rest of the war. Morale was especially poor on the Russian side during this period due to the extremely slow advance combined with previous defeats and the presence of barrier troops. Fighting remained concentrated in the east with particularly bloody fighting occurring at Bakhmut. Utilizing Wagner Group mercenary forces recruited from Russia's prison population, the Russians captured the city at extremely heavy cost. Wagner Group founder, Yevgeny Prigozhin subsequently began to criticize the Russian Ministry of Defence for its lackluster support of frontline troops. In fear of the upcoming Ukrainian counteroffensive, Russia destroyed the Kakhovka Dam and caused flooding downstream in the Dnieper River. The reservoir subsequently dried up. Following this, Ukraine launched their long-awaited counteroffensive which ran into stiff Russian resistance and fortifications. Despite this, Yevgeny Prigozhin's conflict with the Russian Ministry of Defence boiled over and the Wagner Group began to advance against the Russian military establishment, seizing control of Rostov-on-Don and advancing along the M4 highway towards Moscow. Seeing the situation growing out of control, Belarusian president Alexander Lukashenko brokered an agreement with Prigozhin allowing his forces the choice of signing contracts with the Russian Ministry of Defense and safe passage from Russian territory. Prigozhin accepted in fear of Putin's response to his march. However, Prigozhin's stubbornness cost him dearly and he would eventually be assassinated on August 23 when the Russian government detonated a bomb under the wing of his jet. In the meantime, the 2023 Ukrainian counteroffensive failed to break through Russia's defences. The stalemate held across the frontline including at Avdiivka, where Russian offensives were brutally repulsed by Ukrainian fortifications despite the desperate situation on both sides.

Difficulties relating to supplying Ukraine arising from deadlock in the United States Congress began to be felt across the frontline. Russia finally captured Avdiivka after Ukraine withdrew from the city in February 2024. Russia then began a general offensive in 2024 to form a salient which gradually expanded over the following months, capturing several minor settlements including Ocheretyne. This salient developed into a Russian offensive targeting Pokrovsk by July. Likewise Russian forces began to advance on minor settlements such as Chasiv Yar and Toretsk. In response, Ukraine launched an offensive targeting the Kursk Oblast, using conventional Ukrainian forces to attack Russian territory for the first time in the conflict. Minor incursions using pro-Ukrainian forces had been conducted previously into Russian territory, especially in Belgorod Oblast, however with Russian forces entering Kharkiv in the summer of 2024, Zelenskyy made the surprising move of conducting a campaign targeting the Kursk Oblast. The offensive managed to tie down some Russian forces and forced Russia to introduce North Korean combat troops to remove Ukrainian forces from their territory. Russian forces also capture Vuhledar  and Kurakove by year's end.

The diplomatic scene of 2025 saw the United States shift towards the promotion of a peace settlement in the Russo-Ukrainian War following the election of Donald Trump. Trump's efforts alarmed European nations who saw his efforts as too accommodating of Moscow. In February 2025, Trump went so far as to publicly criticize Zelenskyy in the White House. However, Ukraine's relations with the United States were swiftly mended and peace efforts proceeded to focus on bringing Russia to the negotiating table. The subsequent summits at Alaska and Washington, D.C. convinced Trump that peace could be achieved only if Ukraine ceded territory and European nations guaranteed the security of Ukraine. However, the plans presented for ending the war could neither be accepted by European nations supporting Ukraine or the Russian Federation who sought to hold on to the territory it controlled.

In the meantime, Russian forces were frustrated as their optimistic projections of their offensive capabilities was drastically overestimated by the Kremlin. Russia continued a slow but continuing advance in Donbas. Russia captured Velyka Novosilka in January 2025, and Toretsk fell in August of that year. Russia also managed to capture the town of Chasiv Yar but was heavily contested by Ukrainian forces in their advance. However, Russia continued to face difficulties on other fronts. Bloody fighting held up the capture of Pokrovsk in 2025 due to Ukrainian forces being deeply embedded in the town, and the city was not completely captured until January 2026. Likewise, the Russian offensive towards Kupiansk which occurred over the summer and fall was set upon by a Ukrainian counterattack in December 2025.

## '''Syria'''
In 1970, Ba'athist Syria fell under the rule of Hafez al-Assad. Syria was massively impacted by his presidency as he ruled the country with solid foundations for 30 years. Upon his death in 2000, his son, Bashar al-Assad, won an uncontested election. However, by 2011, the Arab Spring began challenging social and political structures in the Arab World, including in Syria. This would prompt foreign governments to lend support to their proxy when war occurred such as in the case of Iran. The nation soon felt the growth of civil uprisings across the country and the formation of the Free Syrian Army and other opposition groups. Organized militias would spring up across the country over the next year.

By 2013, the conflict had escalated into a full-fledged civil war and the opposition's success turned into a stalemate. The Syrian government would launch a number of counter-offensives as the year wore on. At this point in the war, the opposition was composed of many Islamist factions and gave rise to inter-rebel conflict between these factions throughout the war. In 2014, further government offensives and inter-rebel conflict occurred but the rapid establishment of the Islamic State changed the nature of the conflict. The international community would begin actively intervening against the self-declared caliphate. The Americans led the first major intervention against the Islamic State in which coalition member states began a targeted air campaign and involved sending a limited number of special forces personnel into the country. The Southern Front saw a string of victories for the Free Syrian Army while Islamist groups began a takeover of northwestern Syria by 2015. The Islamic State continued gaining ground from government forces in the Tadmur District.

With the war turning against the Syrian government, Assad was forced to turn to Russia to intervene on their behalf. Their brutal air campaign gave the SAA the ability to conduct more successful offensives and they gradually retook the country. However, this also increased tensions between Russia and the west. The war commonly was viewed at this point to have become a proxy conflict between America and Russia. From 2016–2017, the YPG-led Syrian Democratic Forces had decimated the Islamic State in Northern Syria, even wrestling Raqqa from the organization's control. As the Islamic State continued to weaken, Turkey, which was alarmed at the situation, intervened in the conflict against the SDF and began to occupy portions of the country starting when Operation Euphrates Shield commenced.

The Trump Administration would respond to the 2017 Khan Shaykhun chemical attack with a retaliatory missile strike. Tensions between the United States and the Syrian government had escalated to their greatest point in their history at that time. During the year, the CIA finally conceded that they would cut off aid to the opposition and Russia conducted a partial withdrawal of their regional forces with plans that troops would permanently be stationed at their naval facility in Tartus and in Khmeimim Air Base. In 2018, Turkey escalated it's conflict with the SDF by occupying Afrin. Several standoffs emerged between foreign powers following the Douma chemical attack, and several missile strikes conducted by Israel in both May and September. As the Islamic State was losing territory across Syria, the United States began withdrawing it's forces from the country, and a demilitarization zone was established in Idlib by Russia, and Turkey.

The Islamic State lost their final stronghold at the Battle of Baghuz Fawqani and transitioned to an insurgency afterwards. The conflict between the government and the opposition unfroze when the SAA assaulted Hayat Tahrir al-Sham positions in Idlib in an operation the SAA named "Dawn of Idlib". Following an American withdrawal, the Turks launched Operation Peace Spring against the SDF in October, aligning the SDF both with the United States and with the RSII coalition. Following this success, the RSII coalition began "Dawn of Idlib 2" to further constrain opposition factions in northwestern Syria. Turkey retaliated with Operation Spring Shield, which halted further offensives by the Syrian government and their allies. Further conflict focused on engagements between Turkish-backed factions, Hayat Tahrir al-Sham, and the RSII coalition in northwestern Syria. Furthermore, amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, ongoing sanctions, and a liquidity crisis in neighboring Lebanon, Syria faced economic collapse in June 2020, which sparked fears that the Assad regime would also collapse. Efforts were made in order to ensure the status quo remained through to 2023, with increased clashes occurring in northeast Syria. Fighting continued as foreign powers increasingly came to dominate domestic actors in the country.

However, the balance was upset by the Middle Eastern crisis which unfolded following the October 7 attacks by Hamas against Israel. This drew in the Axis of Resistance into conflict with Israel at a time when Assad's government was immensely unpopular and threatened by opposition forces as well as domestic unrest. Hezbollah was immensely weakened by the 2024 Israeli invasion of Lebanon which forced many Hezbollah units to withdraw from Syria to counter Israel's rising power in the Middle East. Hayat Tahrir al-Sham sought to strengthen their position at the government's expense and launched an offensive targeting Aleppo in late November an operation referred to as Deterrence of Aggression. The collapse of government positions sent shockwaves across Syria. Recognizing the weakness of the government, HTS continued their offensive. Likewise Turkish-backed SNA forces along with the SDF tried to pre-empt each other's territorial advances in the Aleppo Governorate, prompting the SNA to launch Operation Dawn of Freedom to prevent the SDF from linking Tel Rifaat to Manbij. With most of Aleppo in the hands of HTS, the group continued their advance to Homa. This prompted Russian airstrikes, which failed to stop the collapse of the SAAF. Hama fell on December 5, which prompted Hezbollah redeployment to Syria. However, with the rate of advance by HTS, Iran wrote off Syria as a lost cause and began evacuating IRGC personnel. This also prompted previously reconciled rebels in southern Syria to once more take arms against the government and capture Suwayda. The SDF seized Deir-ez Zor in the confusion. The American-backed Syrian Free Army took control of Palmyra Homs was outright abandoned by the Ba'athist government by December 8 and Assad's forces were cut off from Syria's west coast, the base of his support. The road to Damascus was wide open and facing this dire situation, Assad and other high profile Ba'athists decided to flee to Russia as Damascus fell into the hands of the rebels. The west coast cities of Lataika and Tartus were both seized shortly thereafter. More than half a century of rule by Assad and the Ba'athist party was ended within two weeks.

## '''Attempted Interference in the 2016 US Presidential Election'''
During the height of the 2016 US Presidential Election, a collection of Democratic National Committee (DNC) emails were leaked and subsequently published by the website WikiLeaks in July 2016. The collection included 19,252 emails and 8,034 attachments from the DNC, the governing body of the United States' Democratic Party. Although Wikileaks did not reveal its source, cybersecurity firms and American intelligence officials stated with "high confidence" that Russian intelligence services were responsible. Most likely believed by many as an attempt to get the Republican presidential candidate Donald Trump elected to the presidency.

During the final weeks of the Election, U.S. intelligence officials warned privately that a campaign they believed was backed by the Russian government to undermine the credibility of the U.S. presidential election, could move beyond the hacking of Democratic Party email systems. Additional efforts by the Russian Government would include posting fictional evidence of voter fraud or other disinformation in the run-up to voting on November 8 2016. This scenario became apparent when news outlets such as Reuters began reporting that both the FBI and U.S. intelligence agencies were examining faked documents aimed at discrediting Hillary Clinton's campaign; as part of a broader investigation into what they believed was an attempt by Russia to disrupt the election.

After Trump's victory in the 2016 election, the US and Russian attempted to forge peaceful ties and coordinate against ISIS in Syria, including withdrawing support to rebel groups who opposed the Russian-backed Assad regime. However, opposition from Congress and within the White House forced the Trump administration to walk back their commitments to Russia, including the US re-affirming the United States' continued commitment to NATO, supporting the Three Seas Initiative, and culminating in the US Congress passing new sanctions against Russia despite presidential opposition. The new sanctions were a potential foreign policy disaster for Russia, as they proved that the Americans could not be coerced into a new relationship and placed serious limitations on Russian oil and gas exports. Russia began cutting ties with the Trump administration after this point, and actively supported anti-American groups along their periphery, including North Korea. In 2018, President Trump refused to implement Congressional mandated sanctions.

## '''New Space Race'''
On the space warfare front, the United States and Russia pursued the development of long-range weapons with which they could strike the territory of the other, and anti-satellite weapons do disable each other's satellite networks. In November 2015, the Russians successfully launched the world's first anti-satellite, and announced a planned Mars manned flyby by 2025. In 2017, the United States began a major buildup of its launch capabilities and satellite defense network and announced a plan to put a human on the surface of Mars in a decade. That same year the US tested a hyper-sonic glide weapon, signaling start of the hypersonic arms race.

### '''2017–2018 North Korea Standoff'''
Tensions along the Korean peninsula flared between 2017–2018, when North Korea successfully tested several missiles with the capability to reach the United States. President Donald Trump and Supreme Leader Kim Jong-un escalated the situation with threats of war. Following the passage of several UN resolutions which called for tighter sanctions on North Korea, Kim Jong-in announced his willingness to restore the Seoul–Pyongyang hotline and agreed to talks with South Korea about North Korean participation in the 2018 Winter Olympics in Pyeongchang. Following several diplomatic meetings between North Korean, South Korean, and American officials, nuclear and ballistic missile tests in North Korea were halted. The meeting between Chairman Kim Jong-un and President Moon Jae-in in April 2018 resulted in the Panmunjon Declaration. President Trump and Chairman Kim met in Singapore on June 12, 2018, resulting in a joint declaration of “full denuclearization of the Korean Peninsula.”

# Final years (2020–2025)
## '''End of the Little Cold War'''
Even before the COVID-19 recession, Russia had already faced a severe economic downturn, the result of both sanctions, and the [decline in oil prices from 2014 to 2015](https://second-renaissance.fandom.com/wiki/Wikipedia:_2010s_oil_glut). Russia's economy managed to rebound from 2016-2017. However, the COVID-19 recession in 2020 exacerbated the fall in oil prices as global consumption of hydrocarbons plummeted even further during an oil price war with Saudi Arabia. This forced the United States to aggressively intervene and demand that Saudi Arabia cut oil production, following which an agreement was struck for both parties to cut oil production.

Upon the election of Joe Biden to the presidency, the US further escalated the Little Cold War. Biden continued the massive buildup of the United States Armed Forces begun under Trump, and implemented new policies towards Russia. In early 2022, Russia's war with Ukraine provoked support for Ukraine from NATO members. The conflict was mismanaged by the Kremlin and resulted in hundreds of thousands of deaths on both sides. However, protests were effectively suppressed by the Russian government. However, demonstrations against the government had re-emerged by 2028. In 2029, dissatisfaction with the Russo-Ukrainian War resulted in a coup d'état which overthrew Putin's regime. The Russian government experienced a sudden and shocking geopolitical collapse, ending the Little Cold War.
```
