## Main-article notes

### Politics, elections, and Mexican-American conflict

- **Deandre Garcia (ID 1562):** Mexican president, December 2132–December 2134, removed by a cabinet no-confidence vote after resisting escalation in the Aztlan conflict. The article describes talks with Thang Levin before the war, public sympathy for Aztlan, her refusal to intervene, and her later pardon and charitable work after the Treaty of San Juan.
- **Aztlan War (ID 1681):** infobox gives 2 February 2133–2134. The narrative says Aztlan forces attacked Camp Navajo after Lionel Halvidar's inauguration; seven southwestern states seceded, while seven other states with similar ancestry remained loyal. Mexico's direct entry is distinguished from earlier support and proxy activity; the page says the Jaso administration began direct engagement in 2134. The article calls the conflict the Third American Civil War and treats it as the beginning of the Third Mexican-American War, although the formal US–Mexico war declaration/invasion came later.
- **Mexican-American Cold War (ID 1597):** gives a common period of 2092–2134 but explicitly says historians disagree on its starting point. It connects the Second Mexican War, LAU formation, border fortifications, Guardiola Incident, European instability, Second Vietnam War, Intermarium, Third Chinese Civil War, India Crisis, Sahara War, Aztlan War, and Third Mexican War. Several headings are placeholders without developed narrative.
- **Treaty of Odessa (ID 1729):** Black Sea Security Treaty concluded in 2034 after semi-secret Poland–Turkey talks, with Japan and the United States assenting. It defined spheres of influence in former Russian Federation territory; the source says public reporting appeared on 1 March 2034 and the treaty faced criticism for limited public debate.
- **Russia (ID 1683):** the infobox dates the Russian Republic 2029–2139, while the prose says it existed 2029–2138 and collapsed after Intermarium's defeat in the Third Mexican-American War. Its history sections for the Russian Empire, Soviet Union, and Russian Federation are headings with little or no text.
- **Balkan Crisis (ID 1700):** identifies the 2049 Bosnia Incident as Turkey's annexation of the Caucasus region of Russia. The narrative describes the Turkish crisis as a diversion while Japan prepared to attack the US Orbital Command Network. The source's timeline and the Treaty of Odessa's 2034 spheres-of-influence agreement should be compared without assuming the latter explains the former.
- **Battle of Brazil (ID 1704):** describes a six-week Mexican/Latin-American invasion beginning 3 May 2129, preceded by Mexico-backed demonstrations and riots in Brasilia. The article says the puppet government signed a non-aggression pact and Mexico established satellite states in southern Brazil. It connects the conflict to the Sahara War, Brazilian debt, and the US–Brazil alliance.
- **Sahara War (ID 1713):** describes a Mexican-backed Tuareg conflict with Nigeria-Arewa, set against US interests in East Africa and the Sahara. A Mexican GPS malfunction leads to a border incident; later Hausa and Azawad rebellions fragment the conflict. The Treaty of Tripoli leaves Nigeria divided and the Tuareg Union internally unstable, while Mali gains a buffer and Brazil gains influence.
- **Belt Pirate Wars (ID 1576):** dates the conflict 1 April 2105–23 June 2127. It begins with 227 Guardiola Specialists and five advanced spacecraft fleeing to the asteroid belt. The 2105 raids, 2107–11 New Menorca/Schubart factions, 2114–15 Lombardo's insurgency, and later remnant piracy are distinguished. The final major battle is in 2115; the longer end date reflects remnant conflicts through 2127.
- **Arctic states (ID 1811):** describes First Nations/Arctic polities formed during the Flood and their opposition to the 2054 Refreeze. The article's First Nations War narrative begins with a 2065 US intervention and a 2067 ceasefire. Nenets partition and Greenland annexation are empty headings; Greenland's remaining population is stated as 870 before annexation.
- **First Nations (ID 1812):** is a redirect to Arctic states, not to the First Nations War article.

### Mars, space infrastructure, and the post-scarcity transition

- **Adrian Hayer (ID 1589):** born 3 June 1995, died 31 October 2144; founder of Galileo Development and first ITC Chairman, 2086–2095. The article covers Lunar Energy, the 2025 lunar mission, Galileo II, Callisto, the 2078 MarsCorp crisis, the ITC takeover, and later lobbying in Washington. The age of 91 at appointment in 2086 is consistent with the stated birth date.
- **First Martian Revolution (ID 1680):** describes the 2077 fee proposal, the 2078 six-month New Richmond sit-in involving 25,000 people, and the 2079 settlement brokered by Galileo Development. The 2081 Rosalind Amendment exempted colonists from Earth immigration restrictions and allowed Mars to divide into territories rather than states. The article describes MarsCorp's dissolution and ITC formation.
- **Space Elevators (ID 1685):** traces the 2035 Molecular Compiler patent and first Earth elevators, the 2038 TransAmerican rail proposal, and a construction process using asteroid-sourced carbon-nanotube ribbons and counterweight asteroids. It says Earth had 13 elevators by the late 2140s, but also says the number would exceed 30 by the dawn of the 22nd century; preserve this chronology as written.
- **NASA (ID 1592):** describes the agency's shift from civil space exploration to warp-capable interstellar missions and first-contact work. Its fleet table identifies IV-103 Constitution as first flown in 2065 and destroyed in 2067; the page states NASA built 25 interstellar vehicles and is testing the New Voyager/Constellation class, with 30 planned ships.
- **O'Neil Ships (ID 1738):** defines colony ships and resource tankers, records the first Titan tanker in 2041, and says 22 ships were completed by the 2070s. Its table includes ship classes, builders, drafts, crew capacities, production windows, and fates. Compare its 22-ship 2070s statement with the earlier Mars-colonization source that reports 19 ships in 2081.
- **Terran diaspora (ID 1739):** says 2 billion people left Earth from 2139–2160, with many returning, and gives three waves: early Mars/Cislunar settlement (2023–2081), mass Martian colonization (2081–2130), and postwar migration averaging 100 million annually after 2139. It says 300 million left Earth in 2150 alone.
- **Sol system (ID 1583):** updates the Solar System with terraformed Venus, Mars, expanded/modified moons, former asteroids as elevator counterweights, depleted asteroid-belt mass, and a sparsely populated Belt. It says Ceres is now Venus's major satellite and Eris is being moved to become Mars's third moon.
- **Residential mall (ID 1720):** says these North American living centers accounted for 48% of US living space in 2160. It describes mall conversions after early-2020s recessions, hyperloop-linked satellite malls, and later 104-story vertical complexes.
- **Bradbury Riots (ID 1671):** one-sentence stub describing a Martian riot for more freedoms.
- **Tenth Party System (ID 1741):** says the system resulted from the merger of the Union and Progress parties into the United Progressive Party.

### Climate, economics, and cultural history

- **List of recessions in the United States (ID 1580):** long chronology from the 20th century through 2156. Several hidden sortable-year values appear copied as 2018 on later rows, including the 2025 recession and 2038/2045/2055-era entries. The Collapse of 2130 row gives September 2130–December 2134 (4 years 3 months) but lists duration as 2 years 3 months. The 2145 recession row lists a 7% unemployment peak in June 2081, apparently carried over from the Second Depression row; the 2156 entry is titled March 2156 Reccession but gives May–July dates and generic notes.
- **Great Reset (ID 1670):** describes a 2008/2010s–2027 era of economic stagnation, inequality, automation, gig work, labor scarcity, resurgent nationalism, race-based violence, and declining agrarian social structures. It says the period ended with the Revolutions of 2027 and the Ocasio-Cortez reforms following the Market Crash of 2027.
- **Crisis of 2130 (ID 1697):** describes the 2130–late-2134 collapse, triggered by a 2128 construction-sector crash, loan losses, the 1 May 2130 failure of Rongji Co-Operative, and exhaustion of small-business loan reserves. Its overview says the US recession ran May 2130–August 2134 (52 months), which differs from the recession-list row's September 2130–December 2134.
- **The Refreeze (ID 1637):** dates the Earth Climate Restoration Effort from 2045. It describes 2054 polar reflectors, the Sahara Ecoregion Project (including mega-lake restoration and 800 million trees by 2055), and aquifer restoration. Aral Sea Project and Rewilding sections are empty headings.
- **Humanity's Garden (ID 1696):** Peter Shay's 2029 nonfiction book advocates active ecological repair, geoengineering, and de-extinction rather than preserving allegedly pristine wilderness. It is cited as an intellectual influence on Postnaturalism and the Earth Working Group.
- **Second Renaissance (ID 1659):** a cultural movement beginning in late-21st-century New Virginia on Mars, with 2091 proposed by some as a precise origin and other accounts dating it to after the Third Mexican War. It spreads through the Solar System and reaches Earth after the war. The article describes QEM-enabled artistic freedom and dynamic perspective.
- **Proclamation of Sapere aude (ID 1690):** Catholic reform movement associated with the Council of Brasília (2054–2066), the Fifth Great Awakening, revised Church structures, gender barriers, religious orders, and a new dogma on Divine Will.
- **The Balkanization Era (ID 1672):** describes Eastern Hemisphere instability after the Guardiola Incident, with disagreement about whether it is a distinct era or part of the Mexican-American Cold War. It links European civil wars and the emergence of the Intermarium.

### Other countries, species, technology, and biographies

- **Korean reunification (ID 1651):** describes the North Korean crisis, international intervention, and the process beginning in 2029. Korean Unity Day is 11 February 2031, although the article says practical reunification continued for decades and only really ended with the Third World War.
- **Korea (ID 1715):** the United Republic of Korea is listed as 1948–2141, with a 2140 population of 53,011,423. It becomes a state in the Association of East Asia during 2140, while the infobox dissolution date is 16 September 2141.
- **Le'ma (ID 1665):** homeworld of the K'lerin, with a 2160 population estimate of 230 million, three named queens, a 55% water surface, and a 642-rotation-per-orbit year. Its biology section describes the Ornizoic era and bird-like but unrelated terrestrial life.
- **Tel-ma (ID 1667):** homeworld of the Tel-bn and a US associated state; the infobox gives a 2160 population of 23,971.
- **Tu (ID 1668):** homeworld of the Moraks; the short page describes two continents, Kolsaviv and Koltumiv, and their geographic zones.
- **Second Great War (Tel-ma) (ID 1791):** dates the war 2075–2087 and describes total war, nuclear/chemical warfare, mutual destruction, and near-extinction of the Tel-bn. The infobox gives combatant strengths of 75.7 million and 69.1 million and casualties of 2 billion and 3 billion.
- **Argommi (ID 1749):** a detailed species/civilization article. It describes the Great Flood on Alo (1202–1338 CE), an estimated 170 million deaths, the Hakloan Renaissance, several confederations, pre-contact technology, and first contact. Odysseus XI reaches YBP1194 on 4 July 2103; Beagle III arrives in 2112; first contact is made in person on 3 June 2113. The speciesbox taxon field says Ornithosapiens cognitus while the article's lead uses Sauropithecus polydactylus; preserve both labels as source content.
- **Liza Maheswaran (ID 1750):** biography of a US Space Force veteran, NASA crew member on the Constitution mission to Tu, Venus Group executive, ITC founder, and folk hero known as the Dragon of Venus. The infobox alt text says “John McCain's official Senate portrait, taken in 2009,” despite the subject being Liza. The page repeats Squirrel Wars in its battle list and describes her death during the 2099 Guardiola Incident.
- **Abdul Şahin (ID 1718):** Turkish president 2044–2054, Islamist nationalist and Pan-Turkist, with an ambition to establish a New Ottoman Empire.
- **Daitaro Sato (ID 1719):** Japanese prime minister 2044–2055, credited with planning the orbital attack that initiated World War III; after the armistice he resigns and commits ritual suicide.
- **Rohan Mustafa (ID 1740):** US Space Force general and Planetary Task Force Pacific commander during the Third Mexican War; infobox gives Chairman of the Joint Chiefs term 2149–2155 and birth in 2079.
- **Podcopters (ID 1661):** small autonomous electric VTOL aircraft, developed from multirotor drones in the late 2010s and used for taxis, medical evacuation, law enforcement, and private transport.
- **Augment Rights Act (ID 1717):** one-sentence stub saying it ended the Falana Act.
- **Energy industry (ID 1593):** generic list of energy sectors; its Talk page asks whether the page is necessary and suggests listing energy sources.

## Election-page inconsistencies

- **2036 (ID 1790):** infobox lists Ivory Toldson as Labor nominee with 43.2%, but the prose says Dylan J. Price and Toldson won as a Labor–Green coalition. The infobox after-election field names Price.
- **2040 (ID 1796):** infobox lists Maya Garcia as Liberal, while the prose calls her the first Conservative Party president and the after-party field says Conservative.
- **2056 (ID 1873):** the article says Dan Forest won through second- and third-choice votes despite Brandon Tran leading the first ballot (40.1% vs 39.9%). This is consistent with the article's ranked-choice explanation, but the prose says Forest promised a single term.
- **2060 (ID 1874):** infobox election_name still says 2056; its before/after fields still name Evalyn Jacobi and Dan Forest, while the prose says Reed Chen won. The result fields therefore appear copied from 2056.
- **2148 (ID 1810):** infobox previous_election is 2144 but previous_year is 2128; title is duplicated; listed vote shares sum to 101%.
- **2152 (ID 1813):** page title is 2152 but infobox election_name says 2156; listed shares sum to 101.2%.
- **2156 (ID 1815):** Dom Terragno is described as the winner; infobox after-party is New Republican while candidate party is New Progressive.
- **2140 (ID 1804):** Cora Fabian is described as the winning vice president and becomes president; infobox has an office field set to Vice President despite the election title being President.
- **2144 (ID 1809):** brief stub says President Fabian won reelection and that this was the first election to include new Latin American states.
