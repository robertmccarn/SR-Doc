### Countries, political integration, and the United States

- **Canada (ID 1896):** Describes Canada as sovereign from Confederation through the Union Act of 2073. Its infobox records a republic date of 2 October 2047 and an estimated population above 40 million in 2070. The article follows the Flood, refugee pressure, Quebec and First Nations secession, and Canada's diversification away from the US toward Europe and Japan. Japan becomes Canada's largest foreign arms supplier by 2042; the source says the US secretly supported secessionist movements after 2042. A 2047 referendum produces a 50.9% majority for a republic. During World War III, Canada initially pursues neutrality while Japanese agents seize control of British Columbia and Yukon infrastructure; the US invades British Columbia, and Cascadia subsequently separates and seeks US Free Association. The article says a 2058 poll found only 38% of Cascadians wanted US statehood. During the First Nations War the US installs a military government in Ottawa; Carla Anderson signs the Union Act on 10 January 2073. Preserve the article's distinction between formal annexation and subsequent territorial arrangements.
- **British Republic (ID 1913):** A parliamentary republic in England and Wales, 2047–2073 in the infobox. The article describes the 2043 Storm Season, collapse of Westminster and the monarchy's legitimacy, US/UN administration, the Winchester Convention, and a 2047 constitutional ratification. Birmingham is the capital. The Republic later enters a Free Association with the US after World War III and is formally annexed under the Union Act of 2073. However, the closing paragraph says England was the last Anglosphere territory to join the Union six years later, after plebiscites. This 2073/2079 distinction requires comparison with the Union Act, England, and British Isles pages. The infobox dates the declaration to 19 May but the prose gives the 2047 establishment without a matching exact day.
- **Republic of Sahtu (ID 1919):** Describes Sahtu as an independent Arctic state until 10 January 2073, with Deline as capital, New Inuvik as largest city, President Grey Sun, and population 579,012. The page says Sahtu was the wealthiest and most powerful of the First Nations, with a large population of British, Australian, Dutch, Dene, and Métis ancestry. Its economy collapsed during the Refreeze; Sahtu joined the other Arctic states in invading Canada and Quebec to obtain resettlement land. The article says the conflict brought US intervention and occupation. The exact independence date and the source's use of “Sahtu” versus “Republic of Sahtu” are not elaborated.
- **Association of East-Asia (ID 1927):** Infobox gives formation on 1 January 2140, Shanghai as capital, population above 931 million in 2160, USD currency, President Raku Li Kim, and Vice President Lin Miyanagi. The article says the association was proposed by Japanese Foreign Minister Hitoshi Yoshida on 9 May 2138, initially as a looser economic/political union, then became a continental federation under US pressure and the postwar occupation. It describes Ha-eun Zaya's Planetary Task Force India conquering and administering much of Asia, installing the Quantum Economic Model, and rebuilding infrastructure. The initial members included Japan, Chita, and Chinese states; Korea and additional Chinese territories joined in 2140, with Southeast Asian expansion in 2147–2149 and further western expansion under Han Jin Wei. The article says the association has 60 provinces and one capital district. Its presidential table lists acting president Arthur Yaeger (2140–2141), Shuu Otomo (2141–2149), Han Jin Wei (2149–2155), Dzu Khanh Pham (2155–2157), and Raku-Li Kim (2157–present); the infobox and table vary the current president's spelling (Raku Li / Raku-Li / Rak-Yi). The article's history contains empty headings and an unfinished sentence in the 2140 formation section.
- **Arabian Association (ID 1933):** Infobox calls the state the Association of Arab States, founded 16 November 2139, with Baghdad as capital, Jeddah as largest city, population above 300 million in 2160, and USD currency. Saul Al-Saud is president and Moham Al-Falasi vice president. The prose is a one-sentence stub. The separate Associated state article lists the Arabian Association as associated since 2140, creating a one-year date difference that should be retained pending clarification.
- **Australia (ID 1937):** Very short former-country article. The Flood brings severe interior drought and threatens coastal cities; the source describes underground cities in the Blue Mountains and says approximately 20 million Australians left for labor opportunities in North America. It gives no government chronology or accession date.
- **Uranus (ID 1988):** Planetary Commonwealth admitted on 12 April 2151; population 56 million in 2160, capital and largest city Herschel, governor Link Chuan, lieutenant governor Harry Rajoelina, and USD currency. The article says Titania and Oberon were tented in 2079, with corporate governance before the 22nd century. Titania has a 4 million population, Oberon 15 million, and nearly 40% of the permanent Uranian population lives in orbitals. The state table lists 14 states/colony clusters. The article discusses resource extraction, helium-3, orbital colonies, and the proposed Tempest Project to dismantle Ariel and Miranda. The infobox links the planetary governor title to “Planetary-Governor of Earth,” which appears to be an incorrect link target. The Titania section contains a malformed sentence (“At present Titania and The largest settlement…”); preserve rather than repair.
- **Central Eurasian Union (ID 1998):** A US associated state founded 16 October 2156, capital Chelyabinsk, largest city Samara, population 91 million in 2160, and USD currency. President and vice-president names are blank. The country-data template for this state (ID 1994) incorrectly gives its alt attribute as “Flag of India.”
- **Associated state (ID 2002):** Defines a free association as a formal relationship in which the United States is the major partner and a political territory with a degree of statehood is the minor partner. The article cites Treaty of San Juan Article X and the Associated Statehood Act of 2139; details vary by state. The table lists the European Association (2139), Association of East-Asia (2140), Arabian Association (2140), Iran (2140), Trans-Siberia (2141), Indonesia (2144), African Commonwealth (2152), Central Eurasian Union (2156), United Nations of Tel-ma (2157), and Federated States of Umknovola (2158). It says foreign-relations independence is recognized under the London Accords for most listed states. A Talk comment asks whether freedom of movement, residence, work, and USD use remain part of the relationship; this is editorial discussion, not an amendment to the article.
- **Flags of the United States (ID 1936):** A long table traces US flag designs from 1775 through 2160. It records 50 stars through 2029, 51 after Puerto Rico's addition, 52 after Guyana, 56 in 2037, 60 in 2066, 78 in 2073, 80 in 2076, 81 in 2090, and 85 in 2133. A black stripe is added in 2043 to memorialize Delaware. The Flag Act of 2091 scales the stars down by 20%; the 81-star design remains in use through 2133. From 2137 to 2151, the design uses seven stars for the planetary commonwealths; from 2151 onward, the page describes eight solid/hollow star elements for Uranus and the Sun. **Internal duration issues:** the 2137–2151 seven-star interval is labeled 17 years despite spanning 14 years; the 2133–2137 85-star interval is labeled one year despite spanning four years. The final 2151–Present row says nine years, reflecting the page's 2160 horizon. The table's 2037–2043 row gives the same 56-star count as the 2043–2066 memorial stripe design.
- **Great Famine (ID 2027):** Global starvation and disease on Earth, 2024–2033, with estimates ranging from 100 million to 500 million deaths. The page distinguishes the Famine of 2024, the Great Famine of 2027, and the American Famine of 2029–2031. It attributes the 2024 event to supply interruptions after the 2022 Russo-Ukrainian War (about 40 million deaths); the 2027 event to the Flood, sea-level rise, loss of arable land, ocean desalination, and shipping disruption; and the American phase to the White Tide's impact on livestock feed production. It says 3.5 billion people depended on rice for 20% of daily calories, 2027 rice production was cut in half, and production did not recover to pre-Flood levels until 2079. The article says the UN agreed in 2027 to reduce GMO barriers and accelerate genetic engineering/cloning research. Several causal-factor headings are empty.
- **Iran (ID 2120):** The infobox gives the Republic of Iran as an associated state from 4 December 2140, population above 103 million in 2160, Tehran as capital, USD currency, Hassan-Ali Estahfani as president, and Abol Sarrans as vice president. The prose calls Iran the last country on Earth to secularize. A presidential table lists Moham al-Ghorbani (2140–2141), Muhammed Estahfani (2141–2145), Abdul-Habibi Sarrans (2145–2153), and Hassan-Ali Estahfani (2153–present). Most historical and demographic headings are empty. The infobox labels Congress as legislature, Consultative Assembly as upper house, and Guardian Council as lower house; preserve those labels as source data.
- **Indonesia (ID 2121):** One-sentence stub: Indonesia is a US associated state. No date, government, or accession history is supplied.

### Mars, ecology, and colonial statehood

- **Flora and Fauna of Mars (ID 2045):** A 23,339-character reference article describing eleven Martian life zones and a deliberately engineered biosphere. It groups wildlife into avisaurs, porpids, dragons, phoenixes, and other modified species, then catalogs regional fauna for Noachia, Margartifer, Acidalia, Amazonis, Hesperia, Elysium, Mariner Valley/East Tharsis, the Australe Plateau/Tharsis Bulge, Hellas, and the Boreal Ocean. Examples include dinosaur-like engineered birds, large filter-feeding porpids, griffins, wyverns, giant tortoises, bison, pollinating moths, and marine predators. The plant section covers Martian Thistle, Ironwood, Black Maple, Arean Poplar, Red Cactus, Longan, Blood Apple, Fire Pear, Red Wheat, Giant Sundew, Nightweed, and Mediplants. Pioneer organisms are described as halophytic extremophytes introduced to build humus, warm the surface, and extract useful soil chemicals. The article says large predators were engineered to avoid humans; Martian Unicorns distribute compounds from the lithosphere but may become extinct within a century without intervention. **Source anomaly:** Ironwood's “estimated maximum height” is given as “300 tons,” a mass unit in a height field. Several names and spellings vary within the source (e.g. Azur/Azure Porpid, Oxian/Oxai Drake, Martain/Martian); do not silently standardize them.
- **New Virginia (ID 2127):** A Martian state with capital Aram, largest city New Richmond, and population 44,934,231 in 2160; admitted to the Union on 1 December 2134. The article says the Praxis Group settled the region in 2031, built the first New Richmond elevator in 2039, and maintained a monopoly over the elevator and colony. The Delegation of New Richmond led representation and anti-Praxis efforts. The 2078 sit-in involved 25,000 people and lasted months; Adrian Hayer/Galileo Development brokered a deal, Praxis sold/ceded holdings, and the ITC gained the colony. The colony was partitioned in 2085, while the New Richmond core remained. New Virginia later favored integration over a second Martian revolution and was among the first Martian states admitted in 2134.
- **Bradbury (ID 2150):** A Martian state with capital Bradbury, population 19,104,321 in 2160, admitted to the Union on 1 December 2134. The article describes Bradbury as the first fully sustainable settlement, an agricultural hub, early shipyard and transit-network center, and later ITC headquarters and political center. It links Bradbury's rise to the early Martian settlement economy, the First Martian Revolution, Adrian Hayer, and the ITC. **Internal chronology conflict:** the overview says first settled in 2027, while the History section says 2024. The overview dates the food-delivery encounter at Meridian Landing to 7 July 2030; the History section gives 7 July 2028. Both passages describe the same three colonists arriving with cheese, honey, milk, bread, butter, and beef, and 15 Meridian residents moving to Bradbury. Preserve both versions.
- **Winter Recession (ID 2043):** One-sentence stub: “The Winter Recession was the worst recession in 40 years.” No dates, geography, or cause are supplied.

Entries include *Skinning the Elephant* (Susan Tuttle, 2034 in this list), *American Empire* (Stephen Michaels, 2031), *Tanning the Donkey* (Sasha Megumi, 2034), *A State of Merit* (Brandon Tran, 2037), *Sushi and Apple Pie* (Carla Anderson, 2087), *Third Manifest Destiny* (2130), *Fukuyama's Black Box* (2159), *Starbound* (2043), *The Great Deluge* (2033), *Generational Chaos* (2071), *Other People* (2048), *The Sagittarius Explosion* (2097), *Inevitable Tetrapods* (2113), *Future of Datasize* (2028), *The End of Civilization* (2148), *The Aerophany*, *The Crisis of Faith*, *Humanity's Garden* (2029), *Inventing the Afterlife*, *The Second Renaissance* (Sean McKnight, 2027), *Through a Dark Mirror* (2160), *The Day They Took My Parents Away* (2092), and *The Brickhead Brothers Trilogy* (2055–2063). The bibliography is explicitly partial.
- **Notable Books (ID 2123):** Redirect to Notable Literature.
- **Skinning the Elephant: The Life and Death of the Republican Party (ID 2135):** Susan Tuttle's nonfiction book, published in 2024, about the Republican Party's decline from the Reagan era through Donald Trump's 2020 defeat. It argues that internet culture made implicit racism more explicit, alienating moderates while consolidating a radical base, and says conservatism later revived through a new party. The source says the Conservative Party was founded less than a decade after the book.
- **Tanning the Donkey: The Life and Death of the Democratic Party (ID 2136):** Sasha Megumi's response to *Skinning the Elephant*, described as published ten years later. It traces Democratic factions from Clinton through the 2020s, Biden's coalition, the 2024 progressive split, and the party's eventual decline as progressives win in 2029 and conservative Democrats join former Republicans in the Conservative Party. The 2034 date in *Notable Literature* is consistent with the source page's “ten years” statement relative to *Skinning the Elephant*'s 2024 date.

It claims accurate predictions of the Second Depression but says Bhattaru wrongly expected only one direct US–Mexico conflict and a US defeat after colonial independence. Its table lays out generations and turnings through the Nomad Generation (2098–2125), with some incomplete rows and labels. Treat the generational schema as the book's in-universe theory, not automatically an objective law of the setting.

### Alien species, first contact, and non-human intelligence

- **First Contact (ID 1920):** Short definition of first contact as one species encountering another species or environment for the first time; in the setting it mainly means humanity's first interaction with extraterrestrial life.
- **Kahi'Umknovola (ID 2040):** Kahi are bipedal pseudo-tetrapods on Umknovola in the 109 Piscium system, adapted to 27% higher gravity than Earth. Their ancestors resemble sea cucumbers; the source describes written civilization dating to 125,000 years and multiple Bronze Age emergences. First Contact is 30 June 2110. The away team arrives on IV-403 *Endurance*, but the same passage calls Michelle Hirono “the Discovery's captain.” By 2160 the species numbers 2.8 billion and joins the American system as a Free Association in 2158. This repeats the ship-name discrepancy already noted in the earlier main-namespace tranche; it is not resolved here.
- **Equoloii (ID 2053):** Sophont species on Equoloa (Kepler-18), with electromagnetic sensory organs and a bipedal, vaguely mammalian body plan. The article describes a long imperial history, including the Aruoqis Empire's use of naturally occurring nuclear fission sources about 6,600 years ago and eventual collapse after radiation sickness and groundwater contamination. First Contact occurred on 10 October 2135 via ISV-18 *Opportunity*. The Gliaceis Empire was initially cautious of human industrial technology because of this history. Humans provide medical aid; the Second Equoloii Industrial Age begins in the 2150s, but a movement to join the US as a Free Association declines by the 2160s due to fears of repeating Aruoqis imperial expansion.
- **Volmarro (ID 2074):** Matriarchal sophonts on Volorum, a moon orbiting the supermassive planet Locno. The page says first contact occurred on 3 May 2125 via ISV-09 *Explorer*. Volmarro civilization has experienced three major collapses associated with stellar flares; contemporary civilization reached the Iron Age again around 2000 CE. First Contact creates tension around gender roles; males gain full education rights and later voting rights in 2153 after political unification. The page says there is no serious movement for Volmarro to join the American protectorate as a Free Association. **Astronomical naming conflict:** the opening places Volorum in the HD 197027 system, while the evolutionary description refers to the 61 Cygni system. Preserve both until the relevant star/planet pages are checked.
- **Hivvir (ID 2101):** Bipedal gliders on Ivrilu in Kepler-452, sharing the planet with the Hiilo. Ivrilu is given 63% of Earth's gravity and 125% atmospheric pressure. The Hivvir's ancestors were six-limbed amphibious cephalopods; the present species has patagia, independent eye movement, and seven color receptors. They live in tree/cliff family groups and have no known writing system. The source says they are more difficult to communicate with than the Hiilo and suggests artificial uplift may be required for written civilization. One sentence spells the related species “Hillo” instead of “Hiilo.”
- **Hiilo (ID 2103):** Related sophonts on Ivrilu, descended from Hivvir ancestors about 700,000 years ago. Unlike the Hivvir they cannot glide and live in temperate savannas. They are about 2.2 m tall; the page estimates 18,000 Hiilo and 70,000 Hivvir at first contact. The Hiilo adopt a written language by 2125 and readily accept human education and agricultural technology, becoming the dominant species on the planet.
- **Abram Kaplan (ID 2113):** Captain of ISV-03 *Constitution* and de facto leader of the Confederation of Free States during the Squirrel Wars/Morak First Contact Wars. The article says he was considered for command of ISV-01 *Enterprise* but NASA selected Jin Musgrave, a civilian scientist/spacecraft commander, for the first human First Contact. Kaplan was reprimanded for his actions on Tu but avoided court-martial, partly due to Musgrave's testimony. He wrote *Contact: An Account of the First Interstellar War*. Most biographical sections are empty.
- **Ckuteko (ID 2170):** Semi-sentient robotic population on Toak in the Kepler-7 system, controlled by eight major supercomputer networks numbered 8, 11, 19, 22, 38, 46, 60, and 74. The droids appear to be remnants of an extinct native species' industrial/mining machines, self-maintaining for nearly two million years after a planetary catastrophe. The article describes rival networks competing for resources and occasionally disabling one another, but says the machines share a root command not to harm organic life. NASA policy is to leave the robotic ecosystem alone, while some researchers advocate disabling it to restore organic ecosystems. This is a useful lead for cross-reading *Other People*, Non-Human Civil Rights, and AIOS/Android pages; do not assume the source has resolved whether the Ckuteko qualify as persons.

## Cross-source continuity leads

1. **Canada / British Republic / Union Act:** Canada and the British Republic both reach formal Union Act annexation on 10 January 2073, but the British Republic page says England's final plebiscite-based accession occurs in 2079. Compare England, British Isles, Union Act, and Union membership pages.
2. **Associated-state dates:** Arabian Association's own infobox says 16 November 2139, while the Associated state list says association since 2140. Keep the dates distinct until reconciled.
3. **East-Asia naming and leadership:** the Association of East-Asia page uses Raku Li / Raku-Li / Rak-Yi spellings for its current president and includes an acting-president period and election/inauguration dates. Compare the country page, presidential biographies, and election pages before selecting a canonical spelling or term.
4. **US flag durations:** the 85-star 2133–2137 row says one year; the seven-star 2137–2151 row says 17 years. The dates imply four and fourteen years respectively.
5. **Bradbury chronology:** the same article gives 2027 and 2024 for first settlement, and 2030 and 2028 for the Meridian Landing food-delivery event. The passages appear to describe the same events.
6. **Volmarro system:** opening says HD 197027; evolution section says 61 Cygni. Check the Volorum/Locno planetary pages and star-system source.
7. **Kahi first-contact ship:** IV-403 *Endurance* is named for the away team, while Michelle Hirono is called the *Discovery*'s captain. Compare the ship biographies and First Contact pages.
8. **Martian ecology:** Ironwood's maximum “height” is given in tons; check whether the source intended mass, height, or a different value. Preserve the source wording until confirmed.
9. **Ckuteko personhood:** their semi-sentience, distributed supercomputer control, self-maintenance, and organic-life protection command should be compared with the setting's personhood framework and AIOS/android legal history.
10. **Election infobox residue:** the 2068 election page has an election_name for 2052 despite a 2068 election date; compare the 2052 election page and adjacent election records.
11. **Generational theory versus canon:** *Generational Chaos* is explicitly WIP and describes its author's predictions, including a failed prediction about the US–Mexico wars. Do not elevate its model or forecasts to objective canon without corroboration.
12. **American annexation and citizenship:** the Talk/Thread discussions raise unresolved distinctions between territory residence, US citizenship, statehood (100,000), and planetary commonwealth status (50 million). The creator's “Reconstruction” analogy is useful context, but the article-level legal framework needs separate sources.
