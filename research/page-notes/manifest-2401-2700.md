## Main namespace — page-level findings

### 2491 — United States Congress
The article describes the 185th Congress (2159–2161), with roughly 1,000 House voting members and 16 Senators representing eight Planetary Commonwealths. It says the Senate has two members per state, then explains that after the 2133 4-Tiered Republic reforms, states were replaced by planetary commonwealths for federal Senate representation. The 172nd Congress (2133–2135) and 35th Amendment are credited with establishing the new intermediate commonwealth tier. Each planet also has its own congressional houses as an off-Earth extension.

Continuity lead: preserve the distinction between historical states and the later Commonwealth representation. The generic statement that each state has two senators sits awkwardly beside the present 16 Senators for eight Commonwealths. The article gives the House a maximum of six two-year terms and Senate a maximum of two six-year terms; check related constitutional/election pages before treating these as settled system-wide rules.

### 2498 — Constructors
The Constructors are the vanished sapient species of Valhalla, a colony moon in the Upsilon Andromedae system. Their radial five-limbed vertebrate ancestry, later secondary bilateral symmetry, five manipulator limbs, and comparatively slow technological progress are described. They apparently disappeared around 1700 AD, less than 400 years before human arrival. A disease outbreak, biological warfare, or evacuation during early spaceflight are offered as theories; the chronoscope is suggested as a way to investigate.

Continuity lead: the chronology is internally plausible if human arrival is in the 21st century, but the actual arrival date and the date at which the chronoscope becomes available should be cross-checked against Valhalla/chronoscope pages. The cause of disappearance is expressly unresolved.

### 2499 — Biotech bubble
The article calls the 2042–2045 speculative boom both the Biotech Bubble and GM Bubble. It connects the Great Famine to relaxed genetic-engineering restrictions and research funding; healthcare labor immigration and education incentives; stem-cell and genetic therapies; cloned/exotic organisms and consumer products; cosmetic biotech; and microgravity biomanufacturing in LEO/GEO. It states that by 2045, 75% of private-clinic revenue came from cosmetic surgery and one-third of clinics had become fully cosmetic biotech businesses. The bubble is said to have crashed in 2045, after which genetic engineering, senescence therapies, and stem-cell technology consolidated. The article links this convergence to the 2060s Biorobotics boom.

Continuity/editorial leads: the space-biotech section says low-volume LEO/GEO operations were already dominated by biotech by the mid-2020s, earlier than the main 2030s/2040s commercialization narrative; clarify whether this was an early niche rather than the mass-market boom. “Cosmic surgery” appears where cosmetic surgery is clearly intended. “GM bubble” and “Biotech bubble” are alternate names in the lead; keep naming consistent across redirects and references.

### 2522 — Biorobotics boom
Also called the Second Biotech Bubble, this is dated roughly 2061–2065 and described as convergence of biotechnology and robotics developed during WWIII. The article discusses epigenetic replacement organs, implanted organs and biorobotic colonies, human augmentation/subcultures, military bio-robotic assault drones, and small commercial “Sprites” or “Daemons.” It says Susuwatari and House Mouse models have been popular since 2144 and that sprite-type biorobots number in the billions by 2160.

Continuity leads: the Androids heading is empty; the Drones heading is populated with military and commercial subheadings. The page calls the same event a boom and a bubble. Cross-check the 2061–2065 period against the end of WWIII and the separate 2042–2045 Biotech Bubble. The assertion that commercial sprites have been popular “since 2144” is a much later market history than the bubble itself, not necessarily a contradiction.

### 2526 — Velminyak
The Velminyak (Sophodracoides lunicolos) are described as the native sophonts of Minya, a habitable moon. They are octopodal, four-eyed, monitor-lizard-like, with four locomotor limbs and four manipulators, two pairs of nostrils, average length of 36 feet and average male/female heights of 17/13 feet. Their reproductive system involves female-laid eggs incubated in a male brood pouch. They are herbivorous ancestors turned omnivorous after the use of fire, have medieval-European-level technology, elected leadership, eight territories, and notable communal architecture using silk secretion as adhesive.

Continuity/science leads: article calls them the Milky Way’s “most primitive” sophonts while describing advanced social and architectural features; define “primitive” as technological rather than cognitive if retained. The claim that the male carries the female’s eggs and “shoots” offspring into the air needs a clearer biological mechanism. The 36-foot length and 17/13-foot height should be distinguished (length vs height) in any comparison. Talk discussion asks that their origin be stated, and the top of the article already identifies Minya.

### 2530 — Minya
Minya is a habitable moon of a gas giant in the Gliese 581 system, around a red dwarf smaller than Sol. It is described as about Ganymede-sized or slightly larger. Its low gravity and energy environment, long eclipse-related dark periods, and low-energy ecology shape the climate and fauna. The article says plant-like organisms have circulatory systems and hearts.

Continuity/science leads: “about one-third the size of Earth” is ambiguous (diameter, radius, area, or mass); Ganymede is substantially smaller than Earth by radius and mass, so specify the measure. Talk discussion explicitly resolves the system choice as Gliese 581, not Alpha Centauri/Proxima; another Talk comment's claim that it is the closest system to Alpha Centauri A is not consistent with the stated system and should remain an editorial comment, not canon. Talk also argues that a Callisto-distance moon with a 17-day orbit would spend about 8.5 days in eclipse and that global rainforests are unlikely; the current article does not actually claim global rainforest, but does describe long dark intervals. The Talk page rejects fresh-water seas because of salt accumulation from river erosion.

### 2585 — Malcolm Moreau
Damian Frederick Holland, pen name Malcolm Moreau, is described as a theriform human with emu-derived traits, born 17 January 2086 in Washington County, Oregon. The article says he became interested in speculative biology at 17, joined the Speculative Evolution forum as Shagrat86, graduated from Jefferson Online College, and received a Linguistics PhD on 14 June 2110. His ten-book Microcontinents series is introduced, but only five titles are listed in this biography.

Continuity leads: the opening gives “Damian” while the physical-appearance section uses “Damien”; the book series page lists ten titles while this biography lists five. The Talk discussion identifies him as one of three people credited with the discovery of Velmin and includes an earlier objection that a 2136 birth date would make him too young to be an explorer; the actual article says 2086. Preserve this as evidence of a corrected/changed date, not a current conflict unless other pages still use 2136.

### 2595 — Vactrain
Defines a vactrain as a maglev train in partly evacuated tubes, with theoretical speeds of 6,400–8,000 km/h. The page references the 2020 Virgin Hyperloop passenger test, a 2021 infrastructure bill, hyperloop-company failures before the 2027 Market Crash, later 2060s/2070s commercial lines in Illinois, Texas, and California, expansion under Oscar Lachman, and nationalization under Lionel Halvidar.

Continuity/editorial leads: the page uses “tracks” and “tracts” inconsistently and says the network lets anyone travel anywhere in the continental US in 30 minutes or less, an extremely strong system-wide claim to compare with route/vehicle limits. Talk comment says the intended modern arrangement may involve cars entering hyperloop pods rather than more conventional concrete roads.

### 2620 — Sovereign Citizen's Alliance
The article describes the SCA as a far-right, white-nationalist, Christian-dominionist terrorist organization and short-lived unrecognized quasi-state during the 2027–2031 American civil conflict. It records the Congress of Sovereign Citizens (2026), rebellion in 2028, territorial activity across the Southeast/Midwest, Mobile and Palm Beach/Palm Springs references, leadership attributed to Donald Trump and Michael/Mike Wallace, mass violence, death camps, forced labor, and eventual defeat by federal forces. The article claims roughly 11 million American deaths resulted from SCA actions.

Major continuity issues to preserve:
- Title is “Sovereign Citizen's Alliance”; infobox formal name is “Sovereign Citizens Alliance”; prose alternates “Sovereign Citizens Alliance” and “Sovereign Citizen's Alliance.”
- Infobox starts 2028 and ends 2031; body says the final SCA members were killed in a seven-day assault on Mobile shortly after AOC took office in 2029, while also describing a later capital in Rapid City through January 22, 2031.
- Infobox first capital says Palm Beach, Florida; prose repeatedly says Palm Springs, Florida. This may be a place-name error or alternate fictional place, but source does not resolve it.
- Body says Palm Springs was captured on 17 October 2029 and leaders had abandoned it; the capital then moved to Mobile and then the Great Plains. Infobox gives Rapid City as the final capital until 2031.
- Infobox deputy is Michael Wallace; prose calls the leader Mike Wallace.
- The flag section says a provisional Bonnie Blue-derived flag was adopted 2 September 2029, while the infobox image filename says SCA Flag (2030–2031).
- The article says the organization was disbanded after the 2029 Mobile assault but also places its formal end in 2031 and discusses death-camp liberation through 2031.
- “Palm Springs, Florida” is also described as the de facto capital; do not silently normalize to Palm Beach.
- The Talk page suggests a name involving “patriots,” but this is editorial preference, not canon.

### 2638 — Tuareg Union
The United Tuareg Tribal Republic / Tuareg Union is dated 2061–2125, declared independence from Mali on 1 September 2061, and arose as Sahara terraforming neared completion under the Earth Working Group. It ended following the Treaty of Tripoli and was reconstituted as the Tuareg Federation after losing southern territory during the Sahara War. Capital is Timbuktu.

Continuity lead: the infobox's coat-of-arms symbol is explicitly “Coat of arms of Mexico,” apparently unrelated to the Tuareg polity. The infobox has blank image_map/image_coat fields, and currency is Mediterranean Franc. Preserve as source defect pending related source checks.

### 2663 — Planetary Guard
The United States Planetary Guard is described as the administrative body for interplanetary-colony militia forces, organized under the Interplanetary Trade Commission rather than the Department of Defense. Formally loyal to the US President but effectively commanded by the ITC Chairman, it was dissolved as a unified organization in 2134; responsibilities went to the US Space Force or US Space National Guard.

Continuity lead: compare the split of responsibilities and command relationship with the ITC, Space Force, and Commonwealth defense-force pages.

### 2670 — Project Rebirth
WWIII-era US-led R&D program with Korean and Chinese scientific support, producing the first post-natal genetic augmentations. The concept came from a 2030s Defense Department think tank; President Jacobi reopened the NSC report and approved DARPA funding in 2049. It moved from enhanced organs and bionic implants toward whole-body genetic treatment, with accelerated work after the Thanksgiving Attacks. Some augmentations became standard among armored troops; later focus shifted from raw strength/speed toward durability and survivability.

Continuity lead: distinguish earlier bionic implants/prostheses from the article's claim about the first post-natal genetic augmentations. Check dates against WWIII (2051–2055), the Thanksgiving Attacks, and the broader Biotech Bubble.

### 2671 — Brazil
The page describes a future Federative Republic of Brazil, the Green Uprising against Bolsonaro after the Flood/Great Famine and 2026 election crisis, a military coup, and Cerrado terraforming that made the region a major agricultural center. It says the Cerrado accounted for 70% of Brazilian farmland by the 2010s and later helped reduce Amazon deforestation.

Continuity/editorial leads: the lead says Brazil is “bordered by the United States” and covers just under 40% of South America; this geopolitical border needs comparison with the US/American Empire map and territorial-history sources. The article says the 2022 election was widely seen as a sham and Bolsonaro remained in power, then says the 2026 election imprisonment triggered rebellion. The text contains “exasperated” where “exacerbated” appears intended, “moritorium,” “loggin,” and an incomplete/awkward phrase about importing soil-nutrient agents “from Space.” Preserve the original text in Bronze.

### 2677 — Sushi and Apple Pie
Autobiography attributed to Carla Anderson, describing her political career, presidency, and views on WWIII. The title refers to her marriage to Heather Suzuki, who was killed in the Thanksgiving Attacks. Anderson supported the necessity of the war but opposed Evalyn Jacobi's censorship of Japanese media. The book also discusses the Anglosphere's annexation, first contact with the K'lerin, Japan trade reopening in the 2060s, and criticism of Oscar Lachman's policies. Talk comment identifies Anderson as the first confirmed LGBT president; treat this as useful editorial context and verify against biography/presidency pages.

Continuity lead: check Carla Anderson's term dates and the Anglosphere/Japan events against the relevant chronology.

### 2689 — Second Biotech Boom
Redirects to Biorobotics boom. The redirect confirms the alternate phrase used in the Biotech Bubble article; the target article calls it the Second Biotech Bubble in its lead.

### 2690 — Europans
Europans (Homo cetimorphis) are described as a heavily modified offshoot of Mermaid/Homo sapiens living in Europa's subsurface oceans. About 40,000 are identified, only one-third estimated born on Europa. They are whale-like, have blubber, luminescent stripes, enlarged eyes, gills instead of lungs, no hair, and depend on introduced fish. At least two settlements exist on the ocean floor. They are classified as “Sentient” and debate rejoining American society.

Continuity/science leads: article calls them a subspecies/offshoot of Mermaid and says some scientists consider them a separate species; category is Species. It uses “Sentient” as a classification without defining the category. The lack of hair is attributed both to drag and predator avoidance; the claim that headdresses are especially popular among women is based on “a few observations.” Cross-check Europa colonization, water demand during the Flood, Mermaid taxonomy, and the meaning of American society in this context.

### 2699 — The Microcontinents: A Natural History
A ten-book field-guide series by Malcolm Moreau about hypothetical ecosystems on prehistoric landmasses with no surviving fossil record. The page lists Tropical Antarctica, Edge of the World, Sundaland, The Great Island, Scale, Feather, and Bone, Erytheia, Atlantis, Lemuria, Middle Earth, and A Forgotten Paradise.

Continuity lead: Malcolm Moreau's biography lists only five titles, and uses “Shell, Feather, and Bone” rather than this page's “Scale, Feather, and Bone.” The book list dates and locations should be preserved as the series' own claims, not treated as independently verified paleontology.
