## Main-article findings and continuity leads

### German successor states
- Hanover, Brandenburg, Rhineland, Saxony, and Bavaria all describe successor republics formed after Germany's dissolution in 2112 and dissolved into the European Association in 2139.
- Hanover's infobox `date_end` is the incomplete string “September 16,” with no year. Other state pages use September 16 without a year.
- Geographic prose and spelling are inconsistent: Hanover spells the neighboring state “Bradenburg”; Saxony uses “Bandenburg”; Brandenburg describes itself as the true successor to Germany. Preserve each source's wording; do not silently normalize.
- Infobox and prose formal names vary (“Republic of the Rhineland” vs “Republic of Rhineland”). Bavaria and Saxony provide border descriptions that should be cross-checked against the corresponding state pages and maps.

### Mexican Vacuum Command
- Lead says the Vacuum Command was initially created in 2090 and became an independent agency in 2113.
- The Project Ahuiateteo section says the Vacuum Command launched the project in the 2080s, predating the stated 2090 creation. This is a direct chronology issue unless an earlier precursor organization is intended.
- The text distinguishes the Vacuum Command, Mexican Terrestrial Command, and Secretariat of Orbital Defense, but sometimes describes the Secretariat as the independent Space Force/commander-in-chief while also describing the Vacuum Command as a separate command structure. Organizational hierarchy needs clarification.
- Project Ahuiateteo's five armed orbitals were unveiled in 2112; Secretariat of Orbital Defense was formally established May 1, 2113. Article says 1,100 human-operated ships and up to one million drones before the Third Mexican War.

### Union of Patria Grande / Latin American Union
- Infobox dates the Union of Patria Grande 2091–2139 and lists Mexico, Venezuela, Colombia, Peru, Paraguay, Argentina, Chile, and Bolivia.
- The article says it was a direct successor to the Union of South American Nations “before World War III,” although the in-universe World War III is dated 2051–2055 and the UPG's own start date is 2091. Wording may describe institutional origins of the predecessor rather than the UPG; clarify rather than infer.
- Current title is “Latin American Union” while the article's formal/common name is “Union of Patria Grande.”

### Mediterranean Union
- Infobox gives 2077–2103; prose says the trade partners formed the union in 2076. Formation date needs reconciliation.
- Infobox member list only shows Egypt and “Tunesia” (spelling as captured); prose describes a wider Mediterranean/North African regional bloc and oligarchic confederacy.
- Infobox status says “Free Trade Union,” while prose emphasizes pseudo-oligarchic governance. These may be compatible but the institutional description should be made explicit.

### United States Army
- The article blends real-world historical material with the in-universe timeline and states that the present-day Army has 5.3 million active soldiers deployed.
- Under “General of the Army,” prose says twelve officers were promoted, but the visible list contains eleven names.
- Under “General of the Armies,” prose says the rank has been held three times, but the list contains four names (Pershing, Washington, Grant, MacArthur).
- These are direct internal count/list inconsistencies. The page also contains partially generic historical material, so distinguish copied history from timeline-specific changes during future synthesis.

### United States state/territory list
- Intro says the United States consists of eight planetary commonwealths; Earth, Mars, Luna, and Uranus sections contain lists, while Venus, Jupiter, Saturn, and Neptune are largely empty/TBA.
- The Ohio row contains malformed table markup and two preceding-entity text fragments, likely shifting the row's cells.
- Territory numbering skips #3 (Micronesia is 1, Melanesia 2, Polynesia 4).
- Several admission dates and predecessor names are captured verbatim and should be cross-checked against the corresponding polity pages. Do not treat formatting defects as canon changes.

### Flora and Fauna of Venus
- Intro states Venus is 50% closer to the Sun than Earth and has 98% of Earth's gravity. These values conflict with Venus's actual orbit (about 0.72 AU) and surface gravity (about 0.90 g); the planet's terraforming does not change either orbital distance or mass/gravity. Flag as factual/worldbuilding error, not silently edit.
- Plant-life section ends with a standalone empty bullet.
- Species descriptions contain editorial typos and inconsistent capitalization, e.g. “one of the most beautiful” typo, “descendent,” “bit” for “bite,” and inconsistent species naming. The source also says large predators are engineered to avoid human odor.

### Population / demographics
- Demographics states Earthsphere population exceeded 14.73 billion in 2160 and projects 18–20 billion by the 23rd century.
- Population growth contains a country-by-country table for 2020–2100. Its linked Talk comment at manifest entry 2364 explicitly says “Ignore the population figures” and asks for cleanup of country names and additions from the timeline. Treat the table's numeric values as provisional/unreliable until reconciled with source-specific demographic pages.
- The Demographics talk comment asks whether the page should be renamed/expanded as “Demographics”; this is an editorial proposal, not an implemented change.

### Other substantive-page notes
- Joaquín Andrade: 72nd Mexican president, 2114–2126; biography says he engineered the India Crisis and renewed hostilities in China. Infobox spelling “Joaquin” omits accent while page title uses “Joaquín.”
- Ir'ba: native intelligent species of Q'jo (HD71334d), described as serpentlike and organized into Paleolithic tribes; do not conflate with Hivvir/Hiilo species from earlier tranches.
- Future of Datasize: very short literature stub attributing the work to Rick Esteban; talk comments speculate about his nationality but do not establish it.
- Third Agricultural Revolution: describes global agricultural changes during the Flood, including Golden Rice 3, Winter Corn, Desert Potatoes, vertical farming, and vat-grown meat. Captured spelling includes “Monstanto.”
- Chemical Element: asserts 172 known elements by 2160, four not yet named; later says elements 165–168 were confirmed by IUPAC in 2158 and named by 2159. Page remains categorized Under Construction.
- Al-Hasa: infobox start year is uncertain (“2055?”); category says “Former counties in Asia” despite the page being a former country. The article uses inconsistent spellings/grammar.
- Oort Cloud: includes a speculative setting detail that dozens of independent/rogue spacecraft inhabit the cloud and may sustain themselves by harvesting ice/helium or piracy.
- People's Republic of China: historical background is largely generic real-world history; future section describes a Second Civil War after flooding/refugee displacement and the Three Gorges Dam collapse. Keep real-world history and in-universe divergence distinct.
- American Empire: in-universe political book advocating an “unintentional empire” interpretation of US power and a tiered doctrine of regional intervention.
- A State of Merit: described as a 2039 work by Brandon Tran that influenced Neo-Jeffersonianism and Conservative Party ideology; its critique of democratic elections and automation-based meritocracy is source content, not neutral fact.
- Tanning the Donkey is categorized “Article for deletion.”
- The Great Deluge and Starbound are brief literature stubs.
- Inevitable Tetrapods discusses convergent evolution and identifies Velminyak, Tel-bn, and Constructors as non-tetrapodal examples.
