## Source-grounded narrative findings

### U.S. politics and the 2132 election

**United States presidential election, 2132 (page ID 636).** The infobox gives election date 4 November 2132 and turnout 80.9%. It lists Lionel Halvidar at 129,783,825 votes / 37.5%; David Castillo at 60,219,694 / 17.4%; Mike Jacobi at 41,876,914 / 12.1%; and Lydia Monroe at 94,482,625 / 27.3%. Those four displayed percentages total 94.3%, and their vote totals sum to 326,363,058. The infobox uses candidate-number slots 1, 2, 4, and 5, with slot 3 absent. The prose says six major candidates were involved, while the displayed infobox has four candidate blocks. The article says Halvidar won a plurality and that his name was not on the ballot in Arizona and New Mexico; its campaign paragraph says his reforms resonated with “the majority” of the electorate, which should not be conflated with a majority of votes when the listed share is 37.5%. Before his inauguration, four Southwestern states declared secession and later formed the United States of Aztlan. The article says the Union had admitted Oklahoma, Jacinto, and Brazos as free-soil states by the 2136 election.

**Thang Levin (page ID 909)** is described as the 64th President (2129–2133), with David Castillo as vice president, and as preceding the Second American Civil War. His presidency narrative centers on the breakdown of the Horizon/Dignidad coalition, unrest in the Southwest, deployment of troops, and secession. The article's own first paragraph calls the conflict the “Third Mexican-American War” in a piped link but labels it “Second American Civil War” in the same sentence; retain both labels as written pending comparison with the relevant war pages.

**Union Party (page ID 912)** gives a founding date of 3 May 2088 in the infobox and says it was founded “around 2088” to unite the colonial delegation to Earth. It traces its origins to the ITC and identifies three Unionist presidents, naming Lionel Halvidar (2133–2141) as the first. This aligns broadly with the 2132 election page's account of Halvidar's election and subsequent presidency, while the exact party-origin date remains an infobox precision versus prose approximation.

### Flood, climate, and the 2027 crisis

**The Flood (page ID 637)** estimates 300 million deaths worldwide and sea levels peaking just under 30 metres. The article's opening places the peak in 2041. Its chronology describes the South Greenland Ice Sheet release in July 2026, with 2.4 billion displaced and 11 million dead in the initial flooding; it then says the Antarctic coastal ice sheets were lost in 2039, driving levels to just under 30 metres, before describing 2041 as the peak. The prose also dates the Summer of Storms to 2043 and says the Flood formally ended in 2065 when sea levels returned to pre-Anthropocene levels. The 2039/2041 sequencing and repeated peak language should be preserved as a chronology issue, not silently harmonized.

**Market Crash of 2027 (page ID 642)** says the crash began on 24 October 2027, but its timeline describes the NYSE reopening on 4 January 2027 after trading was suspended on Christmas 2026, when the Dow fell more than 2,000 points. This is an internal date-order conflict in the article. It attributes the wider crisis to labor shortages, retirement/consumer-demand pressures, housing-market changes, energy demand, and speculative leverage; the effects section says the crash destroyed startup culture and tightened access to venture capital.

**Revolutions of 2027 (page ID 820)** is organized by region and connects the Flood and Great Famine to political upheavals. Its China section describes over 100 million people forced inland by rising seas and Shanghai's protection by a major sea wall; its India/Pakistan section describes famine, unrest, nuclear disarmament assistance, refugee camps, and the eventual independence of Kashmir. Its Brazil section links protests and a military coup to the detention of presidential candidates in the 2026 election. Several country sections are brief or effectively placeholders.

### Mars colonization and China

**Mars Corporation (page ID 638)** gives foundation date 31 December 2023 and dissolution date 1 June 2079. It says MarsCorp landed at Meridian Base on 26 April 2023 after a 27-day voyage, with three ships lifting off from Cape Canaveral in March 2023 and 100 people aboard. It describes a public-private charter, NASA's role, a 100,000-seat sales campaign, and MarsCorp control of land/resources and settlement commerce. This is a direct cross-source date conflict with the previously reviewed **Mars** article (page ID 318), whose crewed-mission chronology places the crewed mission in 2026. Do not choose one date without reviewing all linked expedition pages and preserving both claims.

**United Federation of China (page ID 655)** gives 2058–2108 as its lifespan, describes formation from mainland Social Democrats and Taiwan's Nationalist Unity Society, and narrates its post-Flood recovery, emigration to Mars, economic decline, the Second Vietnam War, and the Third Chinese Civil War. It says the country had over 895 million people shortly before collapse, while its infobox gives 499,121,670 for 2100. That may be a temporal change, but the scale of the difference merits checking against population and emigration sources. The Third Civil War and postwar sections are marked TBA.

**Federal Republic of China (page ID 649)** gives 2108–2139 in its infobox but the narrative contains only a short statement of independence and a TBA Foundation section. The infobox also retains historical Republic of China fields (1912–1949 leaders/events and a 1912 population/area row), a blank end-event date, and the misspelled field label “Presidnet.” It is not yet a substantive account of the 2108–2139 state.

### Conflicts and biographies

**Second Mexican-American War (page ID 821)** dates the conflict 22 June 2090–14 August 2092 and describes Central American and South American theatres, U.S. occupation of Panama, and Mexican efforts to mobilize South American allies. It explicitly connects the dispute to the Great Deportation/Southwestern Crisis and says the unresolved sovereignty question was deferred to the Third Mexican-American War in the 2130s. Its Central American theatre heading is present but has no developed text in the captured page.

**Guardiola Incident (page ID 822)** dates the conflict 1 May 2099–11 February 2100. The infobox lists 103,000 casualties for combatant 1 and 7,600 for combatant 2. The narrative says the Guardiola attack on Glen Station began 1 May 2099 with 8,000 specialists and about 15 assault ships, and says over 100,000 people had been killed by the time Martian reinforcements arrived on 2 September 2099. Its aftermath separately says 7,600 Guardiola specialists/officers were killed, 173 captured, and 227 unaccounted for, with the latter group linked to the Belt Pirate Wars. The different casualty statements refer to different sides/categories in the text and should not be collapsed into a single total.

**Guardiola Foundation (page ID 823)** traces the organization's rise from Mediterranean Union aristocratic interests and Victor Guardiola's arms business to Roma Guardiola's anti-colonial turn. It says the organization was disavowed by the MU in 2098 and links its collapse to the revolutions of 2102. The opening contains the anomalous sentence “In early 2022, a war with a show made the Guardiola Foundation form.” Preserve as a source-text defect/chronology lead; do not infer the intended year or wording.

**Oscar Lachman (page ID 896)** gives birth 21 October 2021 and death 18 April 2136, while the infobox labels his age at death 115. The dates correspond to 114 completed years; record the displayed age as a possible age-calculation issue. His presidency is 2081–2089. The article describes the depression and energy crisis at inauguration, immigration restrictions, deportations under the 2084 Repatriation Act, and later criticism from Carla Anderson. His final quote is “Is it.. is it safe to go now...?”

**Carla Anderson (page ID 902)** gives birth 5 January 2009 and presidency 2065–2073. It connects her administration to the First Nations War, the Union Act of 2073, augmentation rights, and the first human contact with the K'lerin. The page dates the Val're Uprising report to 7 March 2067 and describes the recall of the Enterprise and Constitution crews. Her personal-life section describes her first marriage to Heather Suzuki (2033–2051), later marriage to Archer Moss in 2063, and retirement to Mars in 2108. The First Nations War article (page ID 911) independently gives the conflict's dates and the Union Act annexation outcome.

**First Nations War (page ID 911)** dates the war 1 November 2064–11 May 2067. It says the First Nations invaded Canadian prairie provinces after the Refreeze; the U.S. entered in 2065, initially defensively, then escalated after the 2066 midterms, occupied Calgary, installed a military government in Ottawa, and forced a ceasefire in 2067. The aftermath describes the Spring Truce, the continuing Canadian economic/refugee crisis, and the 2073 annexation of Canada and the rest of the Anglosphere under the Union Act. The article title's “First Nations War,” its alternate names “Canadian Civil War” / “Canadian Crisis,” and the infobox's “Allied victory” should all be retained.
