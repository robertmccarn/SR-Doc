### Political geography and the postwar order

**Poland (ID 939).** The page describes the Federation of Poland and the Baltic as a major Eurasian power and principal Intermarium member. Its infobox gives 2076 as the start year, while the prose says the Federalists formed the Federation in 2077. It gives 2139 as the end year and the Treaty of San Juan as the end event. The article has substantial empty history subsections (Visegrad Group, World War III, founding Intermarium, Second Depression, alliance with Mexico, Third Mexican War), so the captured page is structurally incomplete.

**Intermarium (ID 946).** The infobox calls it a military alliance, dates it 2102–2139, and lists its continent as “The Americas”; the prose explicitly describes an Eastern European military/economic alliance founded on 1 November 2102 under the Second Treaty of Visegrad. Its member list spans Eastern and Central Europe and the former Russian sphere. The geographic infobox field is inconsistent with both prose and membership.

**United States of Aztlan (ID 948).** Infobox dates: 3 December 2132 secession; constitution 15 January 2133; Battle of Camp Navajo 2 February 2133; annexation by Mexico 19 November 2135. The article says the federation was formed by “seven four majority-Mexican states” (source wording), then says four additional states—Colorado, Nevada, Utah, and Panama—declared secession after the civil war began. It describes Utah and Panama as autonomous, Colorado's separatist government being overthrown, and the government-in-exile/shadow government being captured. The text says states declared secession after the November 2132 election, while the infobox gives the secession date as 3 December; it also says Nevada's vote for secession took place on 3 January, with a transition period before Castillo's 20 January inauguration. The capital field says “Santa Fe, then Pheonix” (source spelling). The constitution is described as borrowing from the U.S. and Mexico, strengthening city sovereignty and federal judicial appointments.

**Japan (ID 949).** Infobox gives national existence from 660 BCE to 16 September 2141, with Emperor Hisahito (2089–2141) and Prime Minister Kano Himura (2136–2141) as the last leaders. The narrative traces postwar remilitarization, Sakhalin annexation/lease arrangements, special economic zones, Japanese space development, demographic decline, WWIII participation with Turkey, U.S. orbital bombardment, postwar cultural liberalization, and eventual integration into the Association of East Asia. It describes a 2058 restoration of Article 9 while retaining an interpretation allowing defense of allies. The page contains numerous spelling/grammar defects and abrupt historical transitions; no corrections have been made.

**United States presidential election, 2136 (ID 956).** The election is dated 6 November 2136, with 66.0% turnout. The infobox reports Halvidar 360,437,185 votes / 81.5%; Lydia Monroe 59,261,645 / 13.4%; and Sarima Haden 22,353,195 / 5.1%. The percentages total 100.0%. It says no votes were counted from eleven southern states that joined Aztlan. Halvidar's win is attributed partly to the Battle of Washington and new states admitted from Mars and Luna. Infobox metadata has `office = Vice President` despite `title = President`, and places the election in a presidential-election template. The prose describes Monroe with the pronoun “himself” and identifies Haden as the Progress Party “Peace-wing” candidate.

**David Castillo (ID 958).** The biography gives birth 14 December 2079 and death 27 December 2138. It calls him the 67th and only Dignidad Party U.S. Vice President (2129–2132), later President of Aztlan. It describes his Space Force career, 2111 service aboard USPGS *Shield of Stanford* during the Belt Pirate Wars, 2118 Nevada Senate election, 2132 selection as Aztlan president, unsuccessful wartime leadership, economic collapse/hyperinflation, and death in Mexico City during a food-shortage riot. It uses “Second American Civil War” and cross-links to “Third Mexican-American War” for the same broader conflict; retain the article's own labels.

**Allies of World War III (ID 1110).** The page dates the alliance 2051–2055 and names a “Big Four” of the United States, British Republic, Poland, and Republic of China. It lists NATO, Trans-Pacific Forum, Intermarium, and co-belligerents as affiliated groupings. The Intermarium article (ID 946) dates the organization's formal establishment to 2102, creating a chronology problem if this 2051–2055 list is meant to describe the alliance contemporaneously. The page's affiliated combatant sections are mostly headings with no developed detail.

**African Commonwealth (ID 1185).** The infobox gives founding date 16 September 2139, 2160 population 1.8 billion, capital N'Djamena, largest city Mombasa, and describes a supranational Associated State covering more than half of Africa. The prose describes a staged formation from 2139, handover of occupation territories through 2150, East African accession in 2151, Tanzania and other protectorates joining later, and the Commonwealth reaching its 2155 form. It describes American occupation/protectorate arrangements, the North African Union and Commonwealth of Central Africa as precursor entities, and pressure to integrate remaining states. The member list includes 39 named entries; Abyssinia is explicitly outside the Commonwealth, while Katanga and Zambia are described as prospective members. The text emphasizes that the organization is not a U.S.-style planetary commonwealth.

**European Association (ID 1189).** The infobox gives founding date 16 September 2139, capital Brussels, largest city Istanbul, population 582 million (2160), President Aleksander Paksas, Chancellor Rudolf Verhofstadt, and U.S. dollar currency. The prose calls it a successor to the EU and an Associated State, says Serbia is the only independent sovereign nation remaining in Europe, and describes the U.S. occupation administration and Quantum Economic Model after the Third Mexican-American War. Government text says the President is elected every four years (maximum two terms) and Chancellor every two years (maximum six terms). The succession table has acting President Stark Marino from 17 September 2139 to 20 January 2141, followed by Osk Situ and successors. The table labels the upper house Council of Europe and lower house General Assembly; preserve the terminology as written. A Talk-page exchange at manifest entry 1166–1168 explicitly says these pages are written as if the year is 2160, explaining present-tense language rather than proving a real-world current claim.

**Israel (ID 1162).** The page gives a future history from 1948 through the Flood, Second Nakba, regional realignment, Third Mexican-American War, and U.S. protectorate status. It describes displacement of Palestinians into Sinai camps, the Haifa–Red Sea canal, Negev greenhouses/algae farms, and continuing dispute over Palestinian reparations/repatriation. It gives 2160 survey figures: 57% support some form of reparations and 49% support repatriation for living survivors of the Second Nakba and immediate families. A Talk-page exchange (entries 1140–1144) says the article was revised to fit this timeline and that its version differs from another Second Renaissance Wikia; the discussion explicitly describes the edit as based on an author comment elsewhere. Treat this as editorial provenance, not independent canon.

### Technology, society, and culture

**Trump-Russia scandal (ID 961).** The page describes the 2016 Russian interference/Trump investigation and a 2024 Supreme Court ruling concerning presidential official acts. Several section headings (hacking, social media, media role, money trail, legal actions, Supreme Court, political aftermath) are empty or sparsely populated. The article is marked WIP. It contains date-specific claims but does not provide a complete narrative.

**Virtuals (ID 964).** Defines tactile-neural-interface games and Experience Series. It gives the industry annual sales of 791 billion USD in 2160, traces early VR from DataGlove/Oculus Rift to Neurosystem's 2086 *Sengoku Blade*, smart NPCs and time-dilation learning in the 2090s, and lists virtual titles through 2156. Experience Series allow viewers to inhabit recorded perspectives and emotional states. A Talk discussion in this tranche records the original authors' decisions about Japanese media during/after WWIII, whether mind uploading is possible, and whether Experience Series should be a separate article; one response explicitly distinguishes copying a mind from physically moving a brain into a new body.

**Fifth Great Awakening (ID 1070).** The opening places it in the late 2050s and early 2080s after WWIII, while a later scholarly account says the awakening began in earnest between 2063 and 2075, shortly after First Contact. The article describes religious movements including the Community of Lilu, Enochians, and Magdaleneans, reforms within Catholicism, declining mainline Protestantism, reform-minded Muslim-American institutions, and debate over genetic engineering, non-human rights, and antediluvian history. The source itself questions whether all changes constitute an “awakening.”

**Augment Rights Movement (ID 1074).** The article traces growth in genetic modification from roughly 700,000 modified people in 2040 to 34 million by 2060, the 2058 Warsaw Winter Olympics Nala Jackson scandal, the Falana Act, the 2064 Toray Shears hate crime, *Thatch v. Utah* (5–4), the Splicer Punk phase, and the Augmentation Rights Act of 3 March 2070. It says 72% of U.S. children born by the end of the 2060s were genetically augmented in some way. The page presents a detailed social/legal chronology and cross-links Postnaturalism and the Carla Anderson administration.

**AstroBlues (ID 1079)** is a short description of a 2040s music style developed in lunar and asteroid colonies from blues, jazz, country, R&B, soft rock, pop, hip-hop, and electronic music. Its History section is only “TBA.” **Epical Music (ID 1082)** describes an American art-music style rooted in AstroBlues, arena rock, hard rock, progressive rock, heavy metal, and classical music, with Tactile Neural Interfaces enabling emotionally controlled vocal processing. It gives an Epical period of roughly 2060–2130 but does not develop a detailed period chronology.

**Genetic Engineering (ID 1085).** This is a long overview covering modern genetic engineering, agricultural/industrial uses, terraforming organisms, pets, human augmentation, artificial bodies, cosmetic augmentation, and cloning. It describes the 2027 famine as a turning point for GMO crop adoption; by 2160 GM plants account for 70% of global produce intake. It describes Mars/Luna terraforming microbes, modified livestock and pets, designer babies, post-natal augmentation, and human cloning becoming legal in several countries after mid-century. The page says a U.S. woman used eggs and sperm cells created from her stem cells to clone herself, and lists several historical-figure clones. It also says Lionel Halvidar had 28 million clone orders shortly after his death by the end of 2160. This article supplies broad context for the Augment Rights and Postnaturalism articles.

**Postnaturalism (ID 1215).** Defines a philosophy that gives intelligent life-forms greater moral status than other organisms and rejects the “naturalist fallacy.” It traces the term's modern development through genetic engineering, cloning, bionic augmentation, and the 2027 Olivia Weishaar brain-to-prosthetic-body case. It describes Wesson v. Washington State (2019), state bans on prosthetic bodies, and political/religious debates. It should be read as the source's own philosophical framing, not a neutral external definition.

**Non-Human Civil Rights Movement (ID 1117).** The article calls itself the 2110s Civil Rights Movement and says “Non-Human” is anachronistic terminology for the 2110s. It covers 2108–2115 and the campaigns for legal protections for sentient AIs, androids, and extraterrestrials, culminating in the 35th Amendment. It recounts AIOS and Itskov-type android development, the 2098 ban on creating neural-network AIs, the 2106 AIOS Crisis, Tel-bn discrimination, *Gia-ma v. Chamber of Commerce*, and the 2113 Henry Incident in which two androids create an AI child named Henry, who is then destroyed. It is an important bridge between AI sentience, alien citizenship, and constitutional law.

**Liberal Party (United States) (ID 1136).** Infobox founding date is 7 December 2052, while prose says the party was founded in 2029 through merger of the Labor Party, Working Families Party, and Republican splinters. It describes the Liberals as a long-term third/fourth party and a 2132 coalition partner of Progress/Union; it also says a post-WWIII Liberal-Conservative coalition led to the Horizon Party in 2070. The 2029/2052 discrepancy remains unresolved in the source.

### Alien species and interstellar development

**K'lerin (ID 1138).** Describes *Ornithosapiens cognitus*, the dominant sentient species on Le'ma/Tau Ceti f. It gives average female height 1.5 m and male 1 m, birdlike bipedal anatomy, calcium-carbonate skeletons, a matriarchal six-Queen Val're polity, and hunter-gatherer societies. First Contact is dated 3 January 2065 (Enterprise enters orbit) and 6 April 2065 (NASA authorizes contact/landing). The Val're Uprising in 2067 kills three of six queens; human explorers mediate restoration of a mixed monarchy/representative assembly. Population reaches 230 million by 2160.

**Moraks (ID 1186).** Describes *Scuiranthropus lophops*, the dominant sentient species on Tu/Beta Hydri d. It describes hydrocarbon-fortified bones, non-mammalian reproduction, Bronze Age civilizations around 9000 BCE, and a 2067 First Contact mission by IV-103 *Constitution*. A warp-drive fault causes the ship to emerge near a large Trojan asteroid; its Bubble Trap destroys the asteroid, CRVs scatter the crew, and humans are captured by local authorities/religious orders. Ten crew members are reported dead by the time the IV-101 *Enterprise* arrives. The article says the more open, improvised first-contact approach had a more positive effect than the more cautious Enterprise mission.

**Tel-bn (ID 1145).** Describes *Anthroposepia manipes*, a six-legged, four-armed, squid-like land species from Tel-ma/Kepler 22a. It describes two historical Ages, with the First Great Tel-bn War killing 99% of the species. A second nuclear/chemical war began in 2075 and left the planet devastated. First Contact follows a 2091 gamma-radiation detection and a 2092 arrival by the IV-304 *Spirit*; the article describes the planet as 635 light-years from Earth and a humanitarian fleet taking nearly eight years to arrive. A 2093 asylum request leads to resettlement in Sol, with 40,000 born there by 2105 and 190,000 living there by 2156; only 11,000 elect to return. Tel-ma becomes a U.S. Free Association in 2157. The article explicitly raises concerns about cultural degradation and “Xeno-gentrification.”

### Colonization, planets, and environmental reconstruction

**American colonization of Mars (ID 1139).** Marked WIP. It describes NASA/private-sector cooperation and Mars Corporation's Frontier Program, with SpaceX's 2016 Mars Colony announcement, 2020 Starship development, and early colonization flights. It says the first two biennial launch windows saw 12 Starships with one crewed flight, the third saw 208 ships carrying over 5,000 colonists, and the fourth saw over 500 regular flights. Its early expeditions, MarsCorp and Frontier Program sections are incomplete.

**Luna (ID 1143).** Gives 4 July 2137 as commonwealth establishment and 2160 population of 63 million. It dates the first human settlement to 2019 at the Sea of Tranquility, with a rapid helium-3/mining boom from 2021–2024, Tycho shipyards, and the ASGARD project (three Orbital Command Stations completed/launched in 2037). The article describes the lunar front of WWIII, postwar expansion, and later commonwealth government. This first settlement date and early industrial chronology are important anchors for the Space Age and Mars colonization narratives.

**Venus (ID 1154).** Gives planetary commonwealth admission as 4 July 2137, but says the planet achieved human-rated habitability in 2140. It describes the first human mission in April 2028, solar shield/magnetic shade delivery to L1 in 2036, Lofstrom Loops reversing the planet's rotation by 2054, hydrogen deliveries from 2048, oceans by 2090, and a 24-hour day by 2102. The Terran Diaspora section is only TBA. The 2137 commonwealth admission date precedes the article's stated 2140 human-habitability date.

**Earth Working Group (ID 1106).** Describes a 2045 NGO formed by businesses, NGOs, individuals, and terraforming engineers. It positions Mars Solar Reflectors in Earth polar orbit in 2054 as part of the Refreeze, then builds megalakes, river systems, aqueducts, and desalination facilities in North Africa. Named projects include New Tokyo, Greater N'Djamena, New Shanghai, Wadi al-Rummah, New Dhaka, New Venice, New Zuiderzee, Delmarva, Salton Sink, Port Nukus, and Cairo Restoration. New Shanghai is said to have 2.2 billion residents, described as descendants of people living there at the time; this figure is a strong candidate for cross-check against population pages. The article itself says the Lake Maghreb project was oversized and slowly shrinking through evaporation before being resumed in the 2150s.

**Atlantis (planet) (ID 1217).** Describes the second planet orbiting Alpha Centauri B, with a 2160 population of 653,000 and an acidic ocean/mostly microbial native biosphere before terraforming. First research arrival is 2063; terraforming begins in the late 2070s and is planned to complete by 2160. Native organisms were preserved as lab cultures rather than reintroduced after ocean chemistry was changed. The Nemo Station Incident (2083) involved *Hydromycidium mucophilis*, whose growth in human respiratory passages caused suffocation; damaged ventilation spread the organism, and all 1,080 known station inhabitants were labeled presumably dead. A genetically modified virus was later released to eliminate the organism in the wild. The article's government/political-division section is largely skeletal.

### Conflicts, fleets, and weapons

**Eternal Spring Revolt (ID 1219).** Dates the revolt 4–24 December 2149 aboard the O'Neil ship *Eternal Spring*. The dispute concerns orbital-colony representation and the federal government's transport demands. The ship's residents declare an “Eternal Spring Empire” and attempt to tow the Nemesis asteroid into Earth to cause a KT-scale impact; federal forces capture the ship and disarm the release system. The article reports 25,000 injured and 5,501 killed. It links the revolt to the Max Estavez report and Distributed Territories Act of 2151. The article also describes the 2145 petition for statehood, 2146 departures by five other colonies, and a 2147 failed Space Force capture attempt.

**United States Space Force (ID 1221).** The infobox gives 307,001 active personnel, 177,221 civilian personnel, 69,200 reserve, 105,500 air guard, a $940 billion budget, 1,351 spacecraft, and 55,137 aircraft. The prose says the USSF was formed as a separate branch under the Department of the Air Force on 20 December 2019 and became an independent branch under the Space Security Act on 30 November 2051. It describes consolidation of space responsibilities from the Air Force, Navy, Army, and NRO, WWIII operations, and the postwar build-up. The infobox has a malformed closing structure: its commander/website/aircraft fields appear after the infobox closing braces. The 2051–2160 span is labeled 109 years.

**Olympus-class dreadnought (ID 1223).** Describes 15 km space warships ordered in 2126, with three completed and transferred from colonial Planetary Guards to USSF in 2133. The three named ships were commissioned 22 February 2136. The Mark 19 relativistic railgun is described as firing a 3,000 kg projectile at 275,359,373 m/s, while the text elsewhere says approximately 0.7c; the stated speed is about 0.92c. It also says the gun's maximum firing rate is six shots per minute, but recovery from a single relativistic firing takes over an hour. Both are internal technical/numeric tensions. The ships' final fates are listed as preserved museum ships at Luna, Mars, and Triton.

**Space Age (ID 1232).** Defines a broad mid-20th-to-22nd-century era of exploration, colonialism, post-scarcity economics, and space-power competition. It dates systematic U.S. cislunar development to 2018, Lunar Energy's Moon arrival to 2021, and NASA funding for the first human Mars mission to 2023. It says the Outer Space Treaty was amended in 2025 to allow private businesses to claim/administer off-Earth resources/lands while retaining the ban on nation-state territorial claims. It covers autonomous spacecraft, Starships, in-space servicing, and the growth of interplanetary infrastructure. The 2023 Mars mission date should be compared with the Mars article (ID 318, previously reviewed, which places a crewed mission in 2026) and MarsCorp (ID 638, which gives a 2023 planetfall).

**Hypersonic Weapons (ID 1234).** Defines air-breathing hypersonic munitions and covers X-plane research, drones, hypersonic cruise missiles, and orbital kinetic weapons. It describes X-15 (1961), X-43 (2004), Raytheon AGM-25 Scorpion (2027), and the 2030s–2040s U.S./Japan/Turkey hypersonic arms race. The article contains substantial technical narrative but also typographical defects and incomplete detail in places. The previously flagged “by 2028” Mexican platform date remains a source-faithful continuity lead; do not correct it to a later year.

## Continuity issues and cross-source leads

1. **Intermarium chronology and geography:** ID 946 says 2102–2139 and explicitly Eastern Europe, but its infobox continent is “The Americas.” ID 1110 lists Intermarium as an affiliated World War III grouping in 2051–2055, decades before the formal 2102 establishment.
2. **Poland formation:** ID 939 infobox says Federation begins 2076; prose says 2077.
3. **Aztlan secession chronology:** ID 948 infobox says secession 3 December 2132; prose says the state vote in Nevada took place 3 January 2133, while the federation is said to have formed after the November 2132 election. Also source wording “seven four majority-Mexican states” and “Pheonix” are preserved.
4. **2136 election infobox:** ID 956 has `office = Vice President` despite the article title and `title = President`.
5. **Liberal Party founding:** ID 1136 infobox 7 December 2052 vs prose 2029.
6. **Fifth Great Awakening period:** ID 1070 opening says late 2050s/early 2080s; another paragraph gives 2063–2075.
7. **Mars chronology:** ID 1139 and ID 1232 give 2023 human Mars mission/colonization anchors; ID 638 Mars Corporation says first planetfall 26 April 2023; previously reviewed Mars ID 318 gives a 2026 crewed mission. Preserve all dates until related expedition pages are checked.
8. **Venus admission/habitability:** ID 1154 gives commonwealth admission 4 July 2137 but human-rated habitability in 2140.
9. **Olympus weapon arithmetic/operation:** ID 1223 says 275,359,373 m/s and approximately 0.7c for the same projectile; it also says six shots per minute and over an hour to recover from one firing.
10. **European and African associations:** Both IDs 1189 and 1185 use 16 September 2139 as founding date, while their narratives describe multi-year integration and later accession/handover phases. Treat infobox founding as formal creation and narrative as phased integration unless another source says otherwise.
11. **Alien First Contact cross-links:** K'lerin ID 1138 gives 2065; Moraks ID 1186 gives 2067; Tel-bn ID 1145 gives a 2091 detection and 2092 arrival. These are separate missions/species, not one event.
12. **Atlantis native life and extinction:** ID 1217 says only microbial life was present beyond two kingdoms of plant-like autotrophs, then says all native organisms were preserved as cultures and not reintroduced after terraforming. Nemo Station Incident is a separate later ecological hazard.
