## 1. Binding editorial rule

For all events dated through **2040-12-31**, the setting must remain within authentic real-world scientific, technological, demographic, economic, military, institutional, and geopolitical plausibility. No extraordinary fictional technology or implausible historical rupture is permitted before 2041.

This does **not** mean future events are known facts. The chronology is divided into:
- **Observed baseline (through 2026-11-25):** use verifiable real-world history and current program status. Do not invent or rewrite past events.
- **Plausible scenario (2026-11-26 through 2040):** future developments may be fictional, but must be presented as contingent scenarios with credible mechanisms, bounded scale, and uncertainty. Do not present forecasts as certainties.
- **Post-2040 divergence (2041 onward):** the setting may begin a deliberate alternate-history divergence. The divergence must be dated, explicit, and causally prepared; it cannot retroactively justify impossible pre-2041 events.

"Realistic" does not mean merely grim, nor does it mean every unlikely event is forbidden. It means extraordinary outcomes require extraordinary, independently supported causal chains. A crisis must have a plausible trigger, institutions and actors capable of producing it, geographic limits, logistics, timelines, and measurable consequences.

### Hard prohibitions through 2040

1. No crewed Mars landing, 100-person Mars settlement, 27-day Earth–Mars voyage, permanent Mars colony, planetary terraforming, engineered magnetosphere, redirected asteroid, or industrial Jovian mining economy.
2. No global mean sea-level rise of metres within years or decades; no near-30-metre global rise by 2041.
3. No 2.4-billion-person immediate climate displacement, 300-million-death Flood, or comparable global mortality claim without a defensible demographic model. Under this realism baseline, those source claims are removed, not merely relabelled.
4. No 2027–2048 Second Chinese Civil War, Three Gorges Dam catastrophe as a national-collapse trigger, or 100-million-person Chinese displacement event.
5. No Second American Civil War with multiple seceding states, five captured state capitols, millions of combatants, or multi-million battle deaths. Domestic extremism and political violence may occur at realistic, bounded scales.
6. No 100–500-million-death Great Famine, 2027 global rice-output halving, or uniform worldwide famine. Severe regional food emergencies remain plausible and must be grounded in actual food-system drivers.
7. No dissolution of the Russian Federation in 2029–2038 followed by NATO/UN ground operations to seize nuclear stockpiles. Nuclear command-and-control risks remain a serious concern, but intervention must respect escalation, sovereignty, and force-protection realities.
8. No rapid Turkish occupation of Arabia or Arctic-to-Bosporus megaproject/pipeline absent a credible, independently developed chain of wars, agreements, engineering, finance, and regional consent.
9. No 400-million U.S. population by 2035, overnight retirement-system replacement, wholesale seizure of suburban housing, or instantaneous nationwide implementation of a new political-economic order.
10. No sudden, total global economic reset caused by one demographic variable. Financial crises may occur, but their triggers and transmission channels must be explicit and institutionally credible.

These prohibitions govern the proposed retcon. They do not alter the captured Bronze evidence or accepted canon automatically.

## 2. Real-world calibration and uncertainty

### Climate and sea level

IPCC AR6 gives likely global mean sea-level rise by 2050, relative to 1995–2014, of approximately 0.15–0.23 m under SSP1-1.9 and 0.20–0.29 m under SSP5-8.5. Its 2100 likely ranges are 0.28–0.55 m and 0.63–1.01 m respectively. Low-confidence, high-impact outcomes above the likely range cannot be ruled out, but that uncertainty does not make a 7 m rise in 2026 or nearly 30 m by 2041 plausible. Local relative sea level can differ substantially because of subsidence, uplift, currents, and coastal geography.
- IPCC AR6 WGI SPM: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/
- IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report SPM: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/summary-for-policymakers/

### Food insecurity

The FAO/FSIN 2025 reporting and FAO's 2026 appeal describe acute hunger as concentrated and driven by interacting conflict, weather extremes, economic shocks, inflation, structural fragility, and humanitarian-access constraints. That supports severe regional crises, not an automatic global famine with hundreds of millions of deaths.
- GRFC 2025 September update: https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1749065/
- FAO Global Emergency and Resilience Appeal 2026: https://www.fao.org/emergencies/appeals/global-appeal/en

### U.S. demography

CBO's January 2026 demographic outlook projects the Social Security area population at 349 million in 2026 and 364 million in 2056 under its baseline. This is a defined Social Security population measure, not a generic Census resident-population series. The source's 400-million 2035 figure is therefore removed unless a future, explicit and independently credible demographic scenario justifies it.
- CBO, The Demographic Outlook: 2026 to 2056: https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61994

### Human spaceflight

NASA's 2024 Moon-to-Mars architecture treats lunar exploration as preparation for future crewed Mars missions. NASA's 2024 Mars EDL paper details major unresolved human-landing engineering challenges. NASA's 2025 architecture work continues to define campaign elements, crew, risk, transport, surface power, logistics, and mission architecture; these are planning and trade studies, not a committed date for a human landing. A 2039 Mars architecture study is not evidence of a funded, operational 2039 mission.
- NASA 2024 Moon-to-Mars update: https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-outlines-latest-moon-to-mars-plans-in-2024-architecture-update/
- NASA Mars EDL challenges: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/acr24-compiled-white-papers.pdf
- NASA 2025 Moon to Mars Architecture Definition Document: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250010956
- NASA SAC21 Human Mars Architecture (2039 departure opportunity was an architecture study, not a formal program commitment): https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210026448

### Current spaceflight baseline at the cutoff

NASA's public Artemis material records Artemis II as a crewed lunar flyby in April 2026 and describes subsequent lunar mission steps. Dates in an agency roadmap remain plans subject to schedule, funding, hardware, and safety; they must not be converted into guaranteed outcomes. This alone demonstrates why a 2023 100-person Mars landing is not a realistic baseline.
- NASA Artemis / Moon to Mars: https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/artemis/

## 3. Replacement chronology, 2024–2040

This is a realism envelope and narrative framework, not a deterministic forecast. Events after the cutoff should be phrased as possibilities, policy choices, or developments contingent on specified triggers. Specific future election winners, wars, crashes, and disasters must not be asserted as inevitable. The chronology is multi-causal: climate is one pressure among many, not the default explanation for political, economic, demographic, technological, or security developments. Apply the interaction model and regional scenario cautions in [geopolitical forecast research and multi-factor integration](geopolitical-forecast-integration-001.md).

### 2024 — Historical baseline

- Treat 2024 as a year of real-world geopolitical conflict, economic adjustment, climate extremes, and acute food insecurity in specific conflict- and climate-affected regions.
- Retain the real-world grain, fertilizer, energy, shipping, and humanitarian-access vulnerabilities that can feed into later food-price shocks.
- Remove the source's claim that a discrete 2024 global famine caused 40 million deaths. The captured claim is not a defensible description of observed global mortality.
- No Mars settlement or crewed Mars expedition occurred. Any source text describing one in 2023 is a continuity error to retcon.

### 2025 — Compound pressure, not global collapse

- Climate extremes, heat, floods, drought, wildfire, and coastal damage continue to create regional losses and adaptation costs.
- Acute food insecurity remains concentrated in fragile and conflict-affected regions. Humanitarian funding and access are major determinants of outcomes.
- Governments, insurers, utilities, ports, and municipalities face rising adaptation and resilience costs. Effects vary by region and balance sheet; there is no uniform global insurance collapse.
- Space development remains centered on robotic exploration, commercial launch, satellite infrastructure, and the staged lunar exploration roadmap.

### 2026 — Cutoff-year reality and the end of the fictional Mars premise

- Use actual events through 2026-11-25 as the historical baseline. Do not preserve fictional claims that conflict with verifiable public history.
- There is no 2026 crewed Mars landing. NASA's Artemis II lunar flyby in April 2026 is a real-world crewed deep-space milestone; NASA's later lunar plans remain scheduled objectives, not guaranteed achievements.
- Replace the source's July 2026 South Greenland catastrophe with ongoing observed ice loss and regional climate impacts. There is no seven-metre global mean sea-level jump.
- The term **The Flood** may survive only as a later journalistic or historiographic label for a sequence of destructive floods, storm surges, coastal erosion, river flooding, subsidence, and saltwater intrusion across multiple regions. It is not a single synchronized global inundation.
- Remove the claim that the initial Flood displaced 2.4 billion people or killed 11 million in a single event.

### 2027 — Food, finance, and political stress remain regional and contingent

- Food insecurity can worsen where conflict, drought, extreme heat, export restrictions, fertilizer costs, currency weakness, or blocked humanitarian access overlap. The crisis is geographically concentrated and uneven.
- Remove the claim that global rice production is halved. If a rice shock is needed, identify specific producing regions, crop seasons, weather mechanisms, trade responses, and substitute supply.
- A severe market correction or recession is possible, but an October 24, 2027 global Market Crash is not a predetermined event. If retained as fiction, it requires a traceable trigger (for example, a credit event or sovereign/financial failure) and a documented path through banks, insurers, households, and trade.
- Replace the Second Chinese Civil War's 2027 start with ordinary but serious political, economic, demographic, environmental, and center–province pressures. No Three Gorges Dam failure or nationwide civil war.
- Replace a global wave of revolutions with country-specific protest movements, elections, unrest, repression, negotiated reforms, or leadership changes. Each outcome needs its own local causes.

### 2028 — Constitutional politics, not automatic American civil war

- U.S. political competition proceeds through the constitutional electoral system. Do not predetermine a particular presidential winner or a Seventh Party System as a fact.
- White Tide may exist as a domestic extremist milieu or network, but it must not control state governments or produce multiple secessions. Plausible incidents include threats, isolated attacks, recruitment, online radicalization, local intimidation, and occasional armed standoffs.
- Federal, state, judicial, law-enforcement, and military institutions remain plural and internally differentiated. Their legal authorities and responses matter.
- Canada and Mexico remain sovereign neighboring states whose interests include trade, border management, migration, water, and security. They are not automatically parties to a U.S. civil war.

### 2029 — Adaptation policy and migration

- A national administration may launch a multi-year resilience program: voluntary buyouts, floodplain management, grid hardening, ports and rail upgrades, water infrastructure, disaster insurance reform, housing supply, and targeted public investment.
- Property acquisition must follow existing legal processes: voluntary purchase, negotiated relocation, or specific eminent-domain proceedings with compensation and judicial review. No wholesale seizure of millions of homes.
- Climate migration is cumulative and often internal. Distinguish temporary evacuation, internal displacement, cross-border asylum/refugee movement, repeated displacement, and permanent relocation.
- U.S. population growth follows fertility, mortality, and net migration. The 400-million-by-2035 claim is removed; use current official projections as a baseline and update only when new demographic assumptions are explicitly modeled.
- The AOC presidency is not a required event. If retained as a fictional election result, it must be treated as one possible outcome within normal constitutional politics, not as a causal shortcut to national transformation.

### 2030 — Incremental energy, infrastructure, and geopolitical adaptation

- Energy systems continue a difficult transition involving renewables, storage, transmission, nuclear power where politically and economically viable, fossil-fuel infrastructure, critical minerals, and reliability constraints. No single technology instantly replaces the existing system.
- Governments adapt through industrial policy, trade agreements, permitting reform, grid investment, and competition over supply chains.
- China remains a major state actor. Internal stress, aging, debt, property-market problems, regional inequality, and strategic competition can create policy conflict, but do not automatically cause civil war or disintegration.
- Russia remains a nuclear-armed state. Internal political and fiscal pressures may be significant, but dissolution and foreign seizure of nuclear stockpiles are removed from the pre-2041 chronology.
- Outer-planet exploration remains robotic science and technology development; no commercial Jovian mining economy.

### 2031 — Uneven adaptation and political backlash

- Resilience programs show mixed results. Some cities protect critical assets; others retreat from the most exposed locations. Rebuilding decisions remain politically contested.
- Migration pressures create local disputes over housing, schools, water, labor, and public services. These are manageable policy conflicts, not evidence of a 90-million-person U.S. refugee population.
- Food production and trade adapt through crop breeding, irrigation efficiency, storage, logistics, and market diversification. These changes are incremental and do not eliminate regional hunger.
- Domestic extremist groups may fragment, recruit, or face prosecution; no nationwide armed front is presumed.

### 2032 — Elections and policy cycles

- Elections and party systems respond to real policy outcomes, economic conditions, migration, and institutional trust. Do not impose a predetermined Seventh Party System.
- Long-term health, retirement, housing, and infrastructure reforms proceed through legislation, appropriations, litigation, federal-state bargaining, and phased implementation.
- Any named American Renewal program is a coalition of specific acts and funded projects, not an overnight nationalization or replacement of private retirement arrangements.

### 2033 — No Battle of Salmon as a civil-war endpoint

- Remove the Battle of Salmon as the endpoint of a multi-state civil war. If an Idaho event is desired, it may be a localized law-enforcement incident only if its trigger, participants, casualties, and legal aftermath are proportionate and independently developed.
- The Great Famine does not end in 2033 because no global famine of the source's scale exists in this baseline. Regional food emergencies have separate start/end dates and should be tracked by country and episode.
- Agricultural innovation remains a gradual process shaped by adoption, affordability, regulation, water, soil, and infrastructure.

### 2034 — A contested but functioning international order

- The United States, China, Russia, Turkey, and other major powers remain consequential state actors. Regional conflicts and diplomatic crises are possible; wholesale state collapse is not the default.
- Turkey's regional role can expand through trade, defense exports, diplomacy, energy transit, and selective security cooperation. Remove occupation of Arabia and the Arctic-to-Bosporus mega-pipeline.
- Russia-related nuclear security remains a matter of arms control, monitoring, diplomacy, and contingency planning, not a conventional NATO/UN occupation campaign.
- Climate adaptation remains uneven and expensive. The historical record contains repeated disasters, not a singular planet-wide Flood.

### 2035 — Demographic and fiscal reality

- Use official population projections and explain any deviation with explicit assumptions about fertility, mortality, immigration, and definitions. The CBO 2026 Social Security area population baseline does not support 400 million by 2035.
- Aging societies face pension, health-care, labor-force, and productivity pressures. Responses include later retirement, immigration, automation, training, care-sector investment, and fiscal tradeoffs—not universal full employment.
- Housing and infrastructure programs remain constrained by construction capacity, land, skilled labor, permitting, finance, and local consent.

### 2036 — Technology adoption without magic leaps

- AI, robotics, biotechnology, energy storage, advanced materials, and automation may diffuse substantially, but deployment is constrained by cost, reliability, regulation, energy, supply chains, and organizational capacity.
- No technology is allowed to erase scarcity, eliminate political conflict, or make terraforming and interplanetary settlement routine.
- Space programs can expand robotic lunar/Mars science, communications, navigation, sample-return attempts, and commercial launch services. Crewed Mars remains an ambitious program objective, not an assumed completed mission.

### 2037 — Regional competition and resilience

- Great-power competition continues through trade, sanctions, technology controls, military modernization, alliance management, and regional conflicts.
- No Arctic-to-Bosporus oil pipeline or rapid occupation of Arabia. Major infrastructure corridors require route security, engineering, finance, environmental review, sovereign agreements, and years of construction.
- Coastal retreat, flood defenses, insurance reform, and adaptation funding produce winners and losers; the impacts are regional rather than uniform.

### 2038 — No Russian dissolution as a scheduled fact

- Remove the 2029–2038 dissolution sequence. If Russia undergoes a future constitutional or leadership crisis, develop it from specific domestic institutions, elite alignments, fiscal capacity, regional actors, and military command relationships.
- Do not assume foreign powers can safely seize nuclear weapons or facilities. Any nuclear-security emergency would be a high-stakes crisis with severe escalation and legal constraints.
- China likewise remains intact absent a separately established, credible chain of events.

### 2039 — Space ambitions remain in the architecture phase

- NASA's 2039 Mars-departure concept in SAC21 is a study scenario, not a commitment that a mission will launch in 2039. The setting may depict continued precursor work, propulsion research, cargo planning, radiation protection, life support, surface power, entry/descent/landing, and mission-risk analysis.
- No 100-person crew, permanent settlement, 27-day transit, artificial magnetosphere, terraforming organism, asteroid deorbiting, or planetary solar-mirror array.
- Robotic exploration and a sustained lunar presence are plausible ambitions, but actual schedules depend on program funding, safety, launch cadence, and technical readiness.

### 2040 — End of the realism-constrained era

- Through December 31, 2040, retain real-world physical laws, credible technology readiness, plausible demographics, institutional continuity, and realistic logistics.
- The world may be warmer, more urbanized, older, more automated, more politically strained, and more exposed to climate hazards. It is not yet a post-apocalyptic world, a multi-state U.S. civil-war map, a collapsed nuclear order, or a terraformed solar system.
- Use 2040 as the last year of the realism baseline. Any major departure begins in 2041 or later and must be identified as the point of divergence, with its own causal and technological explanation.

## 3A. Multi-factor geopolitical architecture

The maintainer specifically directed that climate must not be the primary catalyst for the setting's transformation. The 2024–2040 period should instead emerge from overlapping, partly independent pressures: geography and strategic position; political institutions and legitimacy; demographics and migration; industrial structure and supply chains; AI, automation, and digital infrastructure; energy and critical minerals; finance and fiscal capacity; military competition; trade and international institutions; climate and water; public health; culture and information; and science/space infrastructure.

This approach is supported as a useful analytical method—not as a canon forecast—by GPF's integrated model of geography, economy, politics, military power, technology, culture, and neighboring communities, and by RANE/Stratfor's 2025–2035 focus on multipolarity, national self-interest, middle powers, AI, energy transition, space, and cyberspace. See [geopolitical-forecast-integration-001.md](geopolitical-forecast-integration-001.md) for primary-source findings, access notes, and scenario implications.

### Narrative rules

- No single global event should explain every major development. Each regional crisis needs its own proximate causes and local political context.
- Climate hazards should usually act through exposure, infrastructure, insurance, water governance, agricultural conditions, and state capacity. They can amplify other pressures but are not a universal root cause.
- AI and automation should act through compute, electricity, semiconductors, skilled labor, capital, regulation, cybersecurity, and organizational adoption—not as autonomous magic.
- Supply-chain disruption should identify a specific component/material, concentrated supplier or route, inventory buffer, substitution options, and affected downstream industries.
- Demographic aging and migration should affect labor, pensions, health systems, housing, military recruitment, and electoral coalitions gradually and unevenly.
- Geopolitical forecasts should inspire conditional scenarios, never establish that an event has happened or must happen. Forecast claims must be attributed to Friedman personally, GPF, or Stratfor/RANE correctly.
- For major states, preserve competing adaptation and deterioration pathways. Do not choose collapse merely because it is dramatic.

### Regional lenses to carry through the chronology

- **United States/North America:** late-2020s institutional and socioeconomic strain, federalism, generational bargaining, fiscal limits, immigration, AI labor effects, industrial policy, and alliance burden-sharing; no deterministic civil war.
- **Europe:** defense capacity, energy and export dependence, migration, historical borderlands, EU reform, Germany's industrial position, and Poland's growing relative weight; no assumed EU collapse.
- **Russia/post-Soviet space:** strategic depth, resource revenue, sanctions, demographics, military capacity, elite cohesion, and nuclear deterrence; weakness does not equal imminent breakup.
- **China/East Asia:** aging, debt/property adjustment, employment, export competition, technology controls, energy/food security, regional inequality, Taiwan, maritime routes, and Japanese defense posture; tension does not equal civil war.
- **Turkey/Middle East:** geography, defense industry, trade corridors, diplomacy, selective intervention, Syria, South Caucasus, Arab relations, domestic fiscal/political constraints; influence does not mean imperial restoration.
- **Africa/Latin America/Global South:** local agency, debt, mineral value chains, food/fuel imports, urbanization, ports, security, infrastructure, and bargaining among external powers; resource endowment alone does not guarantee power.

### Updated interpretation of the year-by-year chronology

- **2024–2026:** pandemic aftereffects, wars and sanctions, inflation and interest-rate adjustment, industrial policy, AI/compute investment, energy transition bottlenecks, critical-mineral concentration, and climate-related regional losses occur concurrently.
- **2027–2029:** political responses, elections, fiscal constraints, defense burden-sharing, migration politics, industrial subsidies, AI labor impacts, cyber incidents, and supply-chain dependencies interact. A U.S. institutional crisis can be central without becoming civil war.
- **2030–2032:** aging, pension/health costs, automation, power and grid buildout, mineral competition, regional security alignments, and contested trade regimes expose the consequences of earlier decisions.
- **2033–2035:** adaptation is uneven: Europe debates integration and defense; China manages economic/demographic constraints; Japan adjusts defense posture; Turkey exploits selected regional openings; the United States renegotiates its role and social contract.
- **2036–2040:** the order becomes more transactional and technologically contested, but not uniformly collapsed. Compute, electricity, minerals, skilled labor, and trusted supply chains remain strategic assets.

This is a multi-factor scenario architecture, not a prediction that each listed trend will occur in every year.

## 6. Required validation before a canon-edit proposal

A later, separately authorized canon-edit phase must not begin until all of the following are complete:

1. **Dependency discovery:** run exact-title, link, category, infobox, redirect, and text searches for every retired event/entity/number. Distinguish direct links from transclusions and mere text mentions.
2. **Claim ledger:** for every changed claim, record source page/revision, old value, proposed value, evidence, confidence, and all dependent pages.
3. **Timeline consistency:** one canonical event chronology with separate start, escalation, peak, end of organized hostilities, and aftermath dates. Do not overload one infobox date range.
4. **Quantitative review:** mortality, displacement, population, crop production, sea level, military force, casualties, and infrastructure capacity must have definitions, denominators, geography, and time windows.
5. **Institutional/logistical review:** explain legal authority, budgets, staffing, supply chains, command structures, construction lead times, and regional variation for every major event.
6. **Science/technology review:** each capability must have a current readiness state, precursor requirements, energy/mass/logistics requirements, and credible development schedule.
7. **Contradiction sweep:** search all pages for the retired claims and confirm no dependent page preserves the old outcome as fact.
8. **Independent review:** audit the completed proposed diffs against the source archive and this baseline; report unresolved issues rather than silently inventing bridges.

## 7. What remains deliberately unresolved

- This is a qualitative editorial framework, not a validated climate, demographic, economic, military, or geopolitical forecast.
- The user has approved the realism-first direction, not every line of a future fictional timeline and not edits to accepted canon pages.
