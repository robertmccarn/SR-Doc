# Chinese political-history continuity audit — pass 1

Date: 2026-11-20  
Branch: `audit/continuity-research`  
Scope: Raw Wikitext review of People's Republic of China, Second Chinese Civil War, United Federation of China, Third Chinese Civil War, Federal Republic of China, Lost Decade (China), The Flood, World War III, and Third Mexican-American War. The page `China` is a 40-character redirect/short page, not a substantive history article.

## Executive finding

The Chinese succession narrative contains several direct chronological and structural conflicts. The most consequential is that the **Second Chinese Civil War** is dated 2027–2048 in its infobox, while the same article's combatant block labels the PRC side 2105–2111; the later **Third Chinese Civil War** also begins in 2105 and explicitly describes a conflict against the United Federation of China, which did not exist until 2058. The source set appears to have overlapping or misnumbered civil-war narratives, plus an underdeveloped transition from the United Federation to the Federal Republic.

## Confirmed source-level contradictions and defects

### A. Second Chinese Civil War chronology is internally inconsistent

- Infobox: `date=2027-2048`.
- Combatant section includes a `2105-2111` label immediately above the People's Republic of China.
- The article prose attributes the outbreak to the Flood, Greenland ice-sheet loss, displacement, and Three Gorges Dam failure—consistent with the 2027–2048 framing, not 2105.
- Third Chinese Civil War separately starts in 2105 and identifies the United Federation of China as the government being fought.

**Assessment:** The 2105–2111 combatant label in the Second Civil War article is almost certainly misplaced or copied from the Third Civil War chronology. The 2027–2048 infobox is itself broad and the body does not supply a detailed end date. Do not silently change either date without authorial confirmation.

### B. Third Chinese Civil War begins before the state it claims to fight against is established

- Third Civil War begins August 2105.
- It describes forces loyal to the United Federation of China government.
- The UFC is dated 2058–2108, so this relationship is chronologically possible.
- However, the Third Civil War infobox labels the first stage 2105–2111 and the second stage 2117–2126; the UFC formally ends in 2108, while the conflict continues another 18 years.

**Assessment:** Not necessarily a contradiction if the Federal Republic is the successor government continuing the war. But the article does not explain this transition clearly, and the Federal Republic article has no substantive history section. The combatant table labels China as United Federation in 2105–2108 and Federal Republic in 2117–2126, leaving 2108–2117 underexplained.

### C. United Federation of China infobox has inherited/incorrect historical metadata

- `year_start=2058`, `year_end=2108` are consistent with its prose.
- The infobox's `era=20th century` is inconsistent with a 2058–2108 state.
- Several event fields are copied from the Republic of China timeline: Xinhai Revolution (1911), Republic established (1 January, no year), Nanking (1927), Second Sino-Japanese War (1937), Constitution (1947), Huaihai Campaign (1948), and government seat moved to Taipei with no date.
- `event_end` says the seat of government moved to Taipei, while prose says the provisional republic in 2108 was governed from American-run Shanghai. The field may be inherited from the older Republic history rather than a UFC end event.
- The infobox's image-map caption calls the state/territory the Republic of China despite the article's official name being United Federation of China.

**Assessment:** Strong signs of an infobox copied from a historical Republic of China template without replacing legacy fields. The source does not explain whether the UFC deliberately claims institutional continuity with the 1912 Republic or whether these are stale fields.

### D. Federal Republic of China infobox is visibly unfinished and historically misdated

- State dates: 2108–2139.
- First and last leaders are both `BLANK`, with years 1912 and 1949.
- Title typo: `Presidnet`.
- Statistics are labelled 1912, with population 432,375,000, despite the state being a 22nd-century polity.
- Historical Republic of China events (1911–1948) are carried over unchanged.
- `event_end` says seat of government moved to Taipei, without a date, although the state is described as ending in 2139 and succeeding to the Association of East Asia.
- The prose consists only of a one-sentence description and `Foundation: TBA`.

**Assessment:** This page cannot currently support reliable conclusions about Federal Republic leadership, institutions, territorial evolution, or the 2108–2139 transition. Its inherited dates and blank leaders are data-quality defects, not usable canon.

### E. Third Chinese Civil War outcome is inconsistent with UFC article structure

- Third Civil War says China dissolved into more than a dozen new states after major hostilities ceased in 2126.
- UFC infobox says it ended in 2108 and lists the Federal Republic plus numerous successor states (Guangdong, Fujian, Wuyue, Jiangxi, Hunan, Zhuang, Yunnan, Manchuria, Hainan, Taiwan, Federated Provinces of the Yangtze).
- UFC prose has a `Post-Third Civil War` section marked TBA.
- Federal Republic is shown as successor 2108–2139, even though the war's second phase continues through 2126.

**Assessment:** The succession model may intend a provisional Federal Republic to persist through a prolonged conflict while regions secede, but that is not stated or reconciled. The infobox's successor list and war's “more than a dozen states” outcome need a unified account.

### F. Association of East Asia is a claimed successor, but its role is not established in the reviewed source set

- Lost Decade (China) says China was incorporated, along with Japan, into the Association of East Asia after the Third Mexican-American War.
- Federal Republic infobox lists Association of East Asia as successor in 2139.
- Third Mexican-American War lists Federal Republic of China among US allies in 2133–2139.
- The captured page-title list has no substantive article titled exactly `Association of East Asia`; only related File pages and differently named references are present.

**Assessment:** The successor relationship is asserted in multiple places but cannot be validated against an Association's own constitutional/history article in this corpus. Confirm whether the intended page title is `Association of East-Asia`, `Association of East Asia`, or another variant before concluding it is absent.

## Other chronology tension: World War III and the 2048 invasion

The UFC article says Japan invaded Beijing in July 2048. The World War III article dates the global war from November 2051 to January 2055 and also says Japan was already at war in mainland China by 2048. This can be reconciled as a pre-war campaign/occupation followed by global escalation in 2051, but the UFC article's phrasing (“Japanese invasion”) and World War III's chronology should explicitly distinguish the 2048 China campaign from the 2051 global war.

The UFC article says its establishment followed the fall of the Communist regime to internal strife and Japanese invasion; its own founding chronology says the United Front emerged after the invasion of Beijing in 2048. That is broadly compatible, but the PRC page does not document the 2048 collapse or transition.

## High-priority editorial questions

1. Is the Second Chinese Civil War strictly the 2027–2048 conflict, and should the 2105–2111 combatant label be removed or moved to the Third Civil War?
2. What government formally exists from 2108 to 2117 while the Third Chinese Civil War continues? Is the Federal Republic a successor regime, a provisional government, or a government with contested territorial control?
3. Does the Federal Republic remain internationally recognized until 2139 despite the 2126 fragmentation, or is 2139 only the date of its final dissolution/integration?
4. What are the Federal Republic's actual first/last leaders and demographic statistics? The current 1912/1949 values are plainly not 22nd-century data.
5. Is the Association's canonical title `Association of East Asia` or `Association of East-Asia`, and where is its substantive article?
6. Is the historical orthographic projection map intended as a static historical claim map for both the UFC and Federal Republic, or should one or both states have a distinct territorial map?
