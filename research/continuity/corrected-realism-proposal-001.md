# Corrected-realism editorial proposal — 2024–2034

**Status:** Non-canon editorial experiment / proposed alternate-history revision.  
**Branch:** `experiment/corrected-realism`  
**Base:** `audit/continuity-research` at `d93184ad0f78af7b96d264e2bd00d95a8bd61736`  
**Scope:** Major-events timeline, 2024–2034, with implications for linked climate, famine, political, economic, geopolitical, and space-exploration pages.  
**Source archive:** `SR-Doc-Audit.zip`, SHA-256 `d1af0775d69def6ef442b66393e3ad84db6cb6b9ecf9138170573fdfe0a02c8`.  
**Canon status:** Nothing in this proposal changes captured Bronze/Silver data or accepted lore. It is an editorial proposal only.

## 1. Executive recommendation

Retain the setting's central historical direction: climate stress and ecological disruption, food insecurity, migration, economic instability, political polarization, a changing balance of power, and the beginning of a larger space economy. Revise the *mechanisms, scale, and timing* so that the developments emerge through compounding shocks and institutional responses rather than a chain of near-instant global catastrophes.

The existing 2024–2034 timeline is not simply pessimistic; several of its physical and logistical events are incompatible with established science or require multiple independent extraordinary assumptions to occur at once. The most important corrections are:

1. Replace the 2026 seven-meter global sea-level jump with a sequence of severe coastal disasters, storm surges, subsidence, saltwater intrusion, erosion, and managed retreat. Greenland and Antarctic ice loss remains a long-term threat whose commitment and acceleration destabilize planning, not a single iceberg event that raises the global ocean by meters in days.
2. Recast the Great Famine as a decade-long, uneven global food crisis made up of regional famine episodes, export restrictions, conflict, crop failures, disrupted fertilizer and shipping, and unequal access. Keep the title and political consequences; remove the claim that ocean desalination instantly destroys North Atlantic fisheries on a global scale.
3. Rebuild the Market Crash as a compound financial crisis involving property/insurance losses, sovereign and municipal debt, energy and food inflation, credit exposure, and a confidence shock. Aging and labor scarcity are contributing pressures, not a sufficient single cause.
4. Recast White Tide as an escalating domestic insurgency and constitutional crisis, with sporadic organized violence, local militia enclaves, sabotage, attempted seizures, and federal/state confrontation. Do not begin with multiple state governments successfully seceding and fighting a conventional multi-state civil war unless the preceding institutional breakdown is developed.
5. Slow Chinese and Russian fragmentation. Show elite splits, fiscal stress, provincial bargaining, military loyalty problems, and regional autonomy before any state collapse or sustained civil war. A nuclear-armed Russia is not plausibly occupied by NATO/UN forces in a straightforward weapons-seizure campaign.
6. Reframe Turkey's rise as expanding regional leverage, security partnerships, basing, trade, energy transit, and interventions rather than a rapid occupation of Arabia and a single Arctic-to-Bosporus oil pipeline.
7. Move the first crewed Mars landing later, or explicitly retain it as a major alternate-technology premise. The captured Mars sources conflict directly: Mars Corporation page 638 (revision 10394) says three ships carrying 100 people landed at Meridian Base on April 26, 2023; the 2026 major-events timeline calls the 2026 mission Mars Corporation's first manned mission. The colonization page also places aggressive terraforming preparation in 2029, asteroid deorbiting in 2030, and solar mirrors in 2033. A realism-first retcon must revise this entire early-Mars sequence, not merely move one landing date. The 27-day transit, 100-person landing, autonomous habitat construction, L1 magnetic shield, asteroid redirection, and large orbital mirrors are each major technology and logistics divergences.
8. Recast American Renewal as a contested, multi-year package of resilience spending, housing acquisition/buyouts, infrastructure, healthcare, and retirement reform—not the rapid seizure and replacement of millions of suburban homes or the immediate abolition of private retirement arrangements.

The desired result is still a turbulent and frightening decade. It becomes more convincing by making catastrophe cumulative, politically mediated, unevenly distributed, and difficult to govern.

## 4. Proposed revised chronology, 2024–2034

This is the recommended narrative spine. Dates are proposed editorial placements, not claims about what the existing canon already says.

### 2024 — The first food and climate shocks

- Retain 2024 as the beginning of the Great Famine's historical period, but define it as the first of several regional food emergencies rather than a uniform worldwide famine.
- Black Sea trade disruption, fertilizer and fuel costs, export restrictions, drought, heat stress, and conflict reduce food availability and affordability in vulnerable states.
- Humanitarian agencies and governments begin emergency grain procurement and rationing in the most exposed areas. Food insecurity becomes a political issue in states already suffering from weak institutions or debt.
- The captured Great Famine article assigns 40 million deaths to the 2024 episode. A realism revision should not simply erase that figure: either retain it with a transparent regional mortality and excess-death account, or explicitly retcon it and explain why the original historical estimate was inflated. The source does not yet provide that accounting.

**Narrative purpose:** establish that the global food system is fragile before the setting's larger climate disasters. The crisis has multiple causes and different national experiences.

### 2025 — Adaptation becomes a political conflict

- Repeated heat waves, floods, droughts, wildfire seasons, and coastal storm damage raise insurance premiums and expose the limits of local adaptation.
- Insurers retreat from selected high-risk areas or sharply restrict coverage. Municipalities face rising costs for drainage, seawalls, emergency response, and relocation.
- Governments argue over managed retreat, public compensation, rebuilding standards, and who pays for climate adaptation.
- Aging populations intensify labor and fiscal pressures in health care, construction, transport, and elder care. Migration, later retirement, training, and automation partly offset shortages; there is no universal full-employment condition.
- Mars and lunar programs remain in the technology-demonstration and infrastructure-planning phase.

**Narrative purpose:** give the political system visible warning signs and contested policy choices before the crisis peaks.

### 2026 — The Flood becomes a compound coastal disaster

- Keep **The Flood** as the name of a defining historical period, but do not treat the 2026 South Greenland sector event as producing a seven-meter global mean sea-level rise on a short event timescale. The dedicated Flood page says meltwater slowly pushed sea levels toward seven meters; the timeline compresses this into its 2026 event entry.
- A sequence of unusually destructive storm surges, river floods, coastal erosion, land subsidence, saltwater intrusion, and infrastructure failures devastates selected low-lying coastal regions.
- A major Greenland glacier or ice-shelf calving event can produce severe local or regional hazards and draw global attention to ice-sheet instability. It does not by itself add meters to global mean sea level. The Flood article says meltwater 'slowly' raised sea levels toward seven meters; the major-events timeline compresses the description into a 2026 event. The editorial problem is the implied rate and global magnitude, not a claim that the article literally says the entire rise happened in a single day.
- Governments evacuate exposed neighborhoods and ports, impose temporary building moratoria, and begin managed retreat. The most damaging effects are concentrated in vulnerable deltas, subsiding cities, and places with inadequate protective infrastructure.
- North Atlantic fisheries experience severe regional losses from warming, marine heatwaves, ecosystem change, pollution, and disrupted ports. Avoid describing freshwater melt as instant basin-wide desalination.
- Displacement grows through repeated evacuations and livelihood loss. Use cumulative regional totals over years; distinguish internally displaced people, cross-border refugees, people temporarily evacuated, and people whose homes are permanently uninhabitable.

**Narrative purpose:** retain a memorable “Flood” event while making it a compound disaster with a long tail. A single event can still become a global political symbol without physically moving the global ocean by seven meters.

### 2027 — The Great Famine and Market Crash

- The source article itself distinguishes three episodes later grouped under the Great Famine: the Famine of 2024, the Great Famine of 2027, and the American Famine of 2029–2031. Preserve that useful structure. The timeline page gives the overall famine period as 2024–2032, while the dedicated Great Famine page gives 2024–2033; this one-year discrepancy needs an editorial decision.
- The 2024 episode is attributed by the source to the disruption of Russian and Ukrainian grain/fertilizer exports and is assigned 40 million deaths. Treat that as a separate in-universe claim requiring demographic accounting, not as an automatic consequence of the later Flood.
- The Great Famine reaches its most severe phase as the 2024–2026 regional crises overlap with crop failures, export controls, shipping disruption, conflict, high fertilizer prices, and the loss of some productive coastal land.
- Rice-dependent countries face acute stress. The source claims global rice output was halved in 2027 and attributes damage to sea-level rise, loss of arable land, and rice dependency—not only North Atlantic fisheries. Preserve or revise that number only with a regional crop/land-loss model that explains the scale across major rice-producing basins.
- The UN and major agricultural states accelerate drought-resistant crops, crop insurance, seed distribution, fertilizer substitution, food reserves, and controlled research into alternative proteins. These interventions mitigate but do not quickly eliminate hunger.
- The **Second Chinese Civil War** begins in 2027 in the current source chronology (page 1413, revision 11017; infobox date 2027–2048). To preserve that outcome while improving plausibility, portray its opening as a geographically limited conflict following accumulated food, displacement, drought, and legitimacy pressures; make the Three Gorges Dam failure's engineering/operational cause, warning period, downstream impact, and political response explicit. The war then expands or changes character over the following years. Moving the start beyond 2034 is an alternative major retcon, not a minor adjustment.
- The **Market Crash of 2027** occurs on October 24 as the visible break in a financial system already weakened by climate-insurance losses, real-estate repricing, municipal and sovereign debt, food/energy inflation, high borrowing costs, and leveraged exposure. Preserve the source article's speculative boom and extraordinary first-half 2027 financial-sector profit increase as part of the buildup; the crash is a trigger and amplifier, not the inevitable result of Baby Boomer retirement.
- The crash spreads through credit and trade. Some governments impose capital controls, emergency guarantees, bank resolutions, or temporary public ownership of failed infrastructure and insurers.
- Revolutions of 2027 begin in states where food scarcity, corruption, repression, debt, and displacement converge. The movement is international but not uniform: some protests become revolutions, some are suppressed, and others produce negotiated reforms.

**Narrative purpose:** make the famine and crash mutually reinforcing without pretending one variable explains the entire global system.

### 2028 — American election and the beginning of White Tide

- Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez may still win the 2028 presidential election, but the result follows an ordinary constitutional election process and a highly polarized campaign.
- For the realism-first version, White Tide begins as a loose constellation of extremist organizations, armed networks, local political machines, online mobilization, sabotage, threats, and episodic attacks. This is a deliberate scale reduction from the WIP source, which describes 18 state capitols attacked, five captured, three state governments seceding, and later armed insurrection in dozens of states. If those large outcomes are retained, the story must instead depict a genuine multi-state civil war with corresponding federal mobilization and constitutional breakdown.
- A triggering event—an election legitimacy dispute, a federal enforcement operation, a refugee relocation confrontation, or a high-profile political assassination—causes escalation. Select one and develop it; do not rely on the election alone to explain mass secession.
- A small number of local officials, sheriffs, or state legislators cooperate with armed groups. Some militia factions temporarily occupy public buildings or obstruct federal operations, but they do not instantly become recognized successor governments controlling multiple states.
- The federal government, state governments, courts, National Guard leadership, and law enforcement agencies are not monolithic. Their internal disagreements become a central source of danger.

**Narrative purpose:** preserve the rise of White Tide while making its organization, recruitment, escalation, and state response legible.

### 2029 — American Renewal and the refugee question

- The administration introduces **American Renewal** as a contested, multi-year national resilience and recovery program.
- Federal funding supports coastal buyouts, relocation grants, public housing, port and rail reconstruction, grid hardening, flood defenses, water infrastructure, and disaster insurance backstops.
- Rather than seizing millions of suburban homes and replacing them wholesale, government agencies acquire properties through a mix of voluntary buyouts, eminent-domain proceedings for specific infrastructure, public-private redevelopment, and emergency housing programs.
- Preserve the dedicated AOC article's named legislative sequence where possible: National Housing Act (2029), Financial Services Acts (2029 onward), and National Transportation Act (2033), alongside its 'resist, relief, and renewal' framework. Medicare and retirement reforms should proceed through staged legislation, budget negotiations, funding, and federal-state implementation rather than an overnight administrative switch.
- Immigration policy is revised to admit and resettle climate- and conflict-displaced populations. The United States receives millions over several years, with large regional differences and substantial political resistance. The AOC source page separately claims nearly 90 million Americans remained displaced or in refugee camps by January 2029; a realism-first edit must either reduce that number or define what it counts (temporary evacuation, repeated displacement, people in camps, and permanent loss of residence are not interchangeable). A 400-million population by 2035 requires a clearly stated alternate demographic assumption; otherwise use a smaller, still historically significant increase.
- Canadian and Mexican governments become important partners and critics in border management, labor mobility, water, infrastructure, and refugee policy. They are not automatically combatants in an American civil war.

**Narrative purpose:** make American Renewal a politically transformative response whose implementation generates new conflicts and compromises.

### 2030 — Chinese instability and Russian federal stress

- China experiences escalating economic and political instability around the existing war: property and local-government debt, unemployment, food-price grievances, center–province disputes, leadership factionalism, and public anger over emergency measures. These are proposed mechanisms to develop, not all facts established by the captured Chinese Civil War page.
- The Second Chinese Civil War is already underway under the captured 2027–2048 chronology. The 2030 phase should show the conflict's geographic spread, command fragmentation, provincial alignments, and foreign involvement rather than treating the country as merely politically stressed. Some provinces may still negotiate autonomy or remain under central control; the state need not collapse all at once.
- Russia faces a proposed combination of war exhaustion, sanctions, demographic and fiscal pressures, elite succession uncertainty, and regional resentment. Federal subjects bargain for greater autonomy; some local security forces and officials become unreliable. These are candidate causal mechanisms to author, not corroborated claims in the captured Russian Republic page.
- Nuclear security becomes a major international concern. The credible first response is diplomacy, arms-control talks, technical monitoring, and contingency planning—not an uncomplicated NATO/UN ground invasion to seize nuclear weapons from a disintegrating state.

**Narrative purpose:** show the later phases of China's already-running civil war and the separate Russian federal/successor-state crisis without making two enormous nuclear powers collapse simultaneously as a side effect of the same global shock. The proposed 2030 Russian stressors are editorial scaffolding, not claims established by the captured Russia page.

### 2031 — Insurgency, regional bargaining, and adaptation

- White Tide remains active, but the conflict is uneven: attacks on infrastructure, assassinations, armed standoffs, intimidation, and sporadic clashes rather than continuous front lines across several states.
- The government balances prosecution, intelligence operations, political reconciliation, emergency powers, and protection of threatened communities. Abuses and overreach create their own legitimacy problems.
- In China, the civil war continues through shifting fronts, provincial bargains, military defections, and contested supply routes. The Three Gorges Dam failure and its aftermath should remain a specific, sourced causal hinge rather than an unexplained catastrophe.
- In Russia, regional leaders seek fiscal concessions and security guarantees. Foreign powers compete for influence through energy contracts, investment, diplomacy, and security cooperation.
- The Great Famine's acute phase begins to ease in some regions as harvests recover and emergency trade routes, food aid, and agricultural technologies expand. Other regions remain in chronic food insecurity.

**Narrative purpose:** show the slow work of crisis management and political bargaining. A crisis does not end when a headline event ends.

### 2032 — Electoral realignment and a new political settlement

- The 2032 U.S. election confirms a durable realignment. The Seventh Party System can emerge from coalition changes, party splits, regional realignment, and new voting blocs rather than a single constitutional convention.
- American Renewal enters a second phase. Its successes are visible in infrastructure and relocation, while its costs, corruption, uneven benefits, and federal power provoke backlash.
- White Tide factions split between political reintegration, prison-based or underground networks, and irreconcilable violent cells.
- China's 2027–2048 civil war remains unresolved; Russia's federal/successor-state crisis follows its own chronology. Their outcomes are not predetermined by the 2032 U.S. election.

**Narrative purpose:** make political transformation an outcome of several election cycles and institutional adaptation.

### 2033 — The Battle of Salmon and the end of the acute famine period

- Retain the Battle of Salmon, Idaho, as a major turning point. In the realism-first scale-reduced version, frame it as a decisive federal/state operation against a fortified insurgent enclave or connected armed positions. The source describes it as the usual endpoint of hostilities in 2033, while the infobox dates the conflict through 2035 and the aftermath describes federal occupation and raids into the mid-2030s; distinguish combat from postwar security operations.
- The battle weakens the most organized White Tide faction. Residual cells, online recruitment, intimidation, and sporadic terrorism continue beyond 2033.
- The Great Famine's dedicated article ends its period in 2033, while the major-events timeline says 2032. If 2033 is retained as the endpoint, the 2033 chronology can mark the end of the named emergency, with chronic food insecurity and the transition to the Third Agricultural Revolution continuing afterward.
- Agricultural adaptation, trade diversification, improved storage, new crop varieties, and more reliable humanitarian access help restore supply. Do not imply that one new technology instantly solves all hunger.

**Narrative purpose:** deliver a clear historical turning point without an implausibly clean resolution.

### 2034 — A fragile new normal

- The United States is more centralized in disaster response and infrastructure policy, but remains politically divided. The federal system survives, and the legitimacy of emergency powers remains contested. White Tide's principal fighting may have ended at Salmon in 2033, while federal occupation, civil-rights enforcement, and raids continue into 2035 as the WIP article describes.
- Climate migration is a major long-term domestic and international issue. Many people have moved more than once; others remain in exposed places because they cannot afford to leave.
- Food markets have stabilized relative to 2027, but vulnerable states continue to face recurring crises.
- China remains a major power but is still in the 2027–2048 civil war described by the captured page; central authority, territorial control, and foreign involvement should be shown as uneven. Russia's 2029–2038 dissolution is a separate timeline claim and should not be collapsed into the Chinese conflict.
- Turkey is a more influential regional power through energy transit, defense exports, security agreements, and selective intervention. This is the recommended replacement for the timeline's rapid Arabia occupation and Arctic-to-Bosporus pipeline, not an event already corroborated by the captured Turkey article.
- Space development is expanding through lunar infrastructure, robotic Mars missions, orbital logistics, and long-term planning for crewed Mars exploration. A crewed Mars expedition may be scheduled for the later 2030s or beyond; permanent Mars bases and Jovian resource extraction remain later-century developments.

**Narrative purpose:** end the decade with the world transformed but not yet fully replaced. The next decades inherit unresolved crises rather than beginning from a finished post-apocalyptic map.

## 6. Critical continuity decisions

These should be resolved before editing any canon page:

### A. The Flood's magnitude and dates

Page 88 currently says the South Greenland Ice Sheet falls into the Atlantic in 2026 and raises sea levels seven meters. Page 637 describes a near-30-meter peak around 2041, while its chronology also places the Antarctic coastal ice-sheet loss in 2039. The current narrative therefore contains both a physical-timescale problem and a source-internal sequencing issue.

**Recommendation:** preserve the name and political significance of The Flood, but make it a long-running compound coastal disaster beginning in the mid-2020s. Reserve multi-meter global sea-level rise for much later centuries, unless the setting explicitly introduces a non-natural mechanism.

### B. The Great Famine's dates, phases, and mortality accounting

The timeline page 88 dates the famine 2024–2032; dedicated page 2027 gives 2024–2033. The dedicated article explicitly says historians once treated it as three separate events: the Famine of 2024, the Great Famine of 2027, and the American Famine of 2029–2031. It assigns 40 million deaths to the 2024 episode and gives an aggregate range of 100 million to 500 million for the broader period. It also attributes the 2024 episode to the disruption of Russian and Ukrainian grain/fertilizer exports, while later phases involve the Flood, ports, arable land, fishing, rice dependency, and the American livestock-feed system.

**Recommendation:** retain the three-phase structure; reconcile 2032 versus 2033; and publish a transparent mortality model that distinguishes deaths by episode, geography, cause, direct starvation, disease, displacement, and overlap. Do not silently add the 40-million figure to the aggregate range or assume it is excluded.

### C. Mars chronology and the wider terraforming program

MarsCorp page 638 (revision 10394) says three ships carrying 100 people departed in March 2023 and landed at Meridian Base on April 26, 2023; it also names a 2025 New Richmond colony. Timeline page 88 calls the 2026 mission the first manned mission. American colonization of Mars page 1139 (revision 11951) then describes aggressive terraforming planning in 2029, asteroid deorbiting in 2030, genetically modified terraforming agents, and seven large Mars solar mirrors deployed in 2033. These are multiple linked technological divergences, not one isolated date error.

**Recommendation:** choose one coherent path:
- **Realism-first retcon:** move the first crewed landing to a later decade, and also move or substantially redesign the 2025 colony, 2029 magnetoshield/terraforming program, 2030 asteroid deorbiting, 2033 mirrors, and biosphere engineering. The first landing alone cannot be changed without rewriting the downstream program.
- **Lore-preserving exception:** retain the 2023 human expedition and later terraforming milestones, but explicitly treat the propulsion, launch cadence, autonomous construction, planetary engineering, and life-support capabilities as the setting's major technology divergence. The 2026 'first manned mission' line must then be changed to a later expedition or expansion, not another first landing.

### D. White Tide's scale, chronology, and classification

Page 3593 is explicitly marked WIP. Its infobox dates the conflict 2028–2035, lists federal and foreign allies against multiple pretender states, and gives force strengths of 8.9 million and 8.1 million plus 8.55 million and 4.45 million casualties. Its prose calls the conflict the Second American Civil War, says it began with the Columbus Insurrection in late 2028, and treats the Battle of Salmon in 2033 as the usual endpoint of hostilities; the aftermath nevertheless describes federal occupation of much of the Midwest through the 2030s. Timeline page 88 dates White Tide 2028–2031. The AOC article (page 3592) separately claims nearly 90 million Americans displaced by January 2029 and 39 states facing terrorism/armed insurrection by November 30, 2029.

**Recommendation:** first decide whether White Tide is a short insurgency, a multi-sided civil war, or a longer security campaign with a principal combat phase ending in 2033. Reconcile the 2028–2031 timeline, 2028–2035 infobox, and 2033 narrative endpoint. Treat the multi-million force/casualty figures and 90-million displacement claim as high-priority quantitative claims requiring explicit definitions, geographic/temporal scope, and a population/mobilization model. If realism-first reduces the conflict to an insurgency, that is a substantive canon retcon, not merely a wording adjustment.

### E. Russian Federation dissolution and successor-state chronology

Timeline page 88 is the only captured main-namespace source located for the 2029–2038 'Dissolution of the Russian Federation' event and its NATO/UN nuclear-security incursions. Russian Republic page 1683 is a successor-state article: its infobox dates the republic 2029–2139, while its prose says 2029–2138; it attributes the republic's foundation to the collapse of the Union State and Russia's balkanization at the end of the Little Cold War. It does not independently document the nine-year NATO/UN operation or nuclear stockpile campaign.

**Recommendation:** establish a domestic succession/federal crisis first, then a narrowly scoped international nuclear-security crisis. Any foreign deployment should have a specific legal basis, invitation, negotiated agreement, or clear escalation trigger. Reconcile 2138 versus 2139 in the successor-state page if that later period is revised; do not treat the timeline event as a fully corroborated history.

## 7. Tone and narrative principles to preserve

The realism edit should not make the setting bland or optimistic. Preserve:

- The feeling that the early 21st-century international order is losing its ability to absorb shocks.
- The way environmental disasters amplify pre-existing inequalities and institutional weaknesses.
- The interaction between food, energy, finance, migration, and legitimacy.
- The emergence of new political coalitions and regional powers.
- The sense that public institutions can respond effectively in some places and fail catastrophically in others.
- The long arc toward a spacefaring civilization.

Change the explanatory style:

1. **Use compound causation.** Avoid one event explaining every downstream development.
2. **Show lag.** Climate, demographic, institutional, and technological systems respond on different timescales.
3. **Make geography matter.** A coastal disaster affects deltas, ports, and exposed cities differently; it does not affect all countries equally.
4. **Make logistics visible.** Food, fuel, construction materials, transport, skilled labor, and money constrain every large response.
5. **Make institutions imperfect, not absent.** Governments negotiate, improvise, fail locally, and adapt unevenly.
6. **Distinguish insurgency from civil war.** Polarization and political violence do not automatically produce territorial military conflict.
7. **Treat technology as a program, not a magic switch.** Mars settlements and outer-planet industry require launch cadence, supply chains, power, repair, life support, and decades of investment.

## 8. Suggested editing order

If this proposal is approved for a later canon-editing phase, work in this order:

1. **Timeline of major events (page 88):** establish the corrected chronological spine first.
2. **The Flood (page 637):** align physical mechanism, sea-level curve, chronology, and migration scale with the new timeline.
3. **Great Famine (page 2027):** separate regional famine phases, causal mechanisms, and mortality accounting.
4. **Market Crash of 2027 (page 642):** rewrite the financial transmission mechanism and its relationship to the famine and reconstruction.
5. **White Tide (page 3593):** reconcile infobox dates, the 2033 Battle of Salmon, the 2035 end date, and the conflict classification.
6. **Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez / American Renewal (page 3592):** make legislative, budgetary, and implementation stages visible.
7. **Mars Corporation / American colonization of Mars (pages 638 and 1139):** resolve the 2023/2026 contradiction and set a technically credible mission sequence.
8. **Russia, Turkey, China and related geopolitics:** update each dedicated article only after the revised causal chain and chronology are agreed.
9. **Re-run continuity checks:** verify every date range, event reference, infobox, timeline entry, and cross-page relationship after edits.

Do not begin editing Bronze source pages as part of this proposal. Any future canon revision should be a separately authorized operation with explicit page scope and a preserved before/after record.

## 9. Limitations and open decisions

- This is a qualitative plausibility edit, not a quantitative climate, demographic, macroeconomic, or military simulation.
- IPCC projections are global mean sea-level ranges; local relative sea level can differ due to subsidence, uplift, currents, and regional ocean dynamics.
- World Bank climate-migration estimates are scenario-dependent and focus on internal migration, not all forms of displacement.
- CBO's 357-million 2035 figure is the report's Social Security area population measure, not a generic interchangeable population series. The projection is a baseline under specified laws and assumptions, not a prediction that binds an alternate history.
- The 2024–2034 revised chronology is not a full retcon specification for every downstream page. Mars terraforming, the Second Chinese Civil War, White Tide, and the Great Famine each have dependent chronology and scale claims that require synchronized revision.
- Civil-war assessments are contingent and should not be read as guarantees about future political stability.
- The current captured source may contain additional pages, Talk discussions, and later chronology claims that have not been exhaustively reviewed for this editorial proposal. The proposal uses selected main-namespace pages and a bounded 2024–2034 scope. Exact-title manifest review found no dedicated main-namespace articles for the timeline's 'Rise of Turkey' or 'Dissolution of the Russian Federation' events; the latter is represented in the timeline and a later Russian Republic successor-state article.
- The Flood article's 300-million death estimate and 2.4-billion displacement claim require a defined accounting basis. The 2.4-billion figure is attributed to the initial flooding, but the source does not say 'immediately'; the proposal now avoids that extra timing claim.
- The White Tide and AOC articles make much larger claims than the timeline summary: WIP White Tide lists 8.9m/8.1m force strengths and 8.55m/4.45m casualties; AOC's article says nearly 90m Americans were displaced and 39 states faced terrorism/armed insurrection by November 2029. These are not validated by external sources and require in-universe population, mobilization, and casualty accounting.
- The 2029–2037 American Renewal article already contains staged legislation (including housing, financial-services, and transport measures). The realism proposal should preserve those named policy milestones where possible, while revising the administrative/legal mechanism and capacity assumptions.
- The Mars colony article's 2029–2033 terraforming sequence is downstream of the 2023 crewed landing. A realism-first retcon must move or redesign those milestones, not just change the first mission date.
- Before canon editing, the maintainer should decide whether the Mars 2023 landing is a deliberate technological divergence or an error to retcon, and whether the Flood is a literal global sea-level event or a historical label for a sequence of coastal disasters.

## 10. Bottom line

A more realistic Second Renaissance does not need fewer crises. It needs crises that unfold through credible mechanisms and on credible timescales. The recommended version keeps the 2024–2034 decade as a period of extraordinary upheaval, but changes the story from “the world ends in a series of sudden events” to “multiple stressed systems fail in sequence, interact, and force political adaptation.” That makes the setting more internally coherent and, arguably, more unsettling.
